# Research question supplement

96 additional formulations in 16 domains. Some refine existing questions; none is newly implemented by this research.

## Patient value over a full care cycle

**R-VALUE-01 — Which pathways look inexpensive in claims but consume excessive patient time, caregiver time or unreimbursed clinical work?**

Data: Patient/caregiver time diaries; staff resource use; claims; longitudinal outcomes.
Failure to catch: Do not equate charges or paid amounts with resource costs.
Owner: Clinical outcomes lead and patient representatives. Sources: FOUND01, FOUND02, INT03.

**R-VALUE-02 — Which patients meet disease-control targets while their function or ability to live independently is worsening?**

Data: Repeated function and symptom reports; disease measures; care goals.
Failure to catch: A normal disease metric must not override deteriorating function.
Owner: Clinical outcomes lead and patient representatives. Sources: FOUND01, FOUND02, INT03.

**R-VALUE-03 — Where do apparently excellent patient-reported outcomes reflect missing responses from the sickest patients?**

Data: Survey invitations and responses; baseline severity; language; follow-up status.
Failure to catch: Complete-case averages cannot be presented as the whole population's outcome.
Owner: Clinical outcomes lead and patient representatives. Sources: FOUND01, FOUND02, INT03.

**R-VALUE-04 — Which separate disease programs impose conflicting instructions or excessive cumulative treatment burden on the same person?**

Data: Program enrollment; instructions; medication schedules; patient preferences.
Failure to catch: Individually valid disease rules can conflict at patient level.
Owner: Clinical outcomes lead and patient representatives. Sources: FOUND01, FOUND02, INT03.

**R-VALUE-05 — Which recovery trajectories improve time at home without transferring unsustainable work to an unpaid caregiver?**

Data: Residence timeline; caregiver burden; patient preference; utilization.
Failure to catch: More days at home alone is not evidence of better care.
Owner: Clinical outcomes lead and patient representatives. Sources: FOUND01, FOUND02, INT03.

**R-VALUE-06 — Which clinically equivalent pathway options better match an individual's priorities for function, symptoms and convenience?**

Data: Validated pathway options; outcomes; elicited preferences; resource costs.
Failure to catch: Do not infer preferences from age, spending or diagnosis.
Owner: Clinical outcomes lead and patient representatives. Sources: FOUND01, FOUND02, INT03.

## Whether an intervention actually helped

**R-CAUSAL-01 — Would the apparent success of our high-utilizer program remain if untreated patients selected at the same utilization peak were compared?**

Data: Eligibility-time utilization; comparison cohort; intervention dates; follow-up.
Failure to catch: Regression to the mean can produce impressive before-after improvement.
Owner: Evaluation scientist and clinical sponsor. Sources: EVAL01, EVAL02, EVAL04, ENG04.

**R-CAUSAL-02 — Which subgroup benefits are prespecified and replicated, and which emerged only after searching the data?**

Data: Analysis plans; subgroup definitions; effect intervals; replication cohort.
Failure to catch: A post hoc subgroup is a hypothesis, not an established targeting rule.
Owner: Evaluation scientist and clinical sponsor. Sources: EVAL01, EVAL02, EVAL04, ENG04.

**R-CAUSAL-03 — Which apparent intervention effects disappear when attribution is fixed at baseline rather than updated after patients move?**

Data: Attribution history; deaths; disenrollment; provider changes.
Failure to catch: Selective attrition can make performance look better.
Owner: Evaluation scientist and clinical sponsor. Sources: EVAL01, EVAL02, EVAL04, ENG04.

**R-CAUSAL-04 — Where do intervention and comparison groups have too little overlap to estimate who benefits reliably?**

Data: Baseline features; assignment mechanism; treatment exposure.
Failure to catch: Do not extrapolate a treatment effect to unsupported patients.
Owner: Evaluation scientist and clinical sponsor. Sources: EVAL01, EVAL02, EVAL04, ENG04.

**R-CAUSAL-05 — Could spillover to untreated clinics be contaminating our program comparison?**

Data: Shared staff; patient movement; referral networks; rollout timing.
Failure to catch: A comparison site exposed to the intervention is not an untreated control.
Owner: Evaluation scientist and clinical sponsor. Sources: EVAL01, EVAL02, EVAL04, ENG04.

**R-CAUSAL-06 — Which proposed program can be evaluated through a feasible randomized or phased rollout before widespread investment?**

Data: Operational capacity; equipoise; eligibility; outcome frequency; implementation schedule.
Failure to catch: A sophisticated observational estimator does not substitute for an available stronger design.
Owner: Evaluation scientist and clinical sponsor. Sources: EVAL01, EVAL02, EVAL04, ENG04.
