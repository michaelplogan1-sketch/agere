# Executive, cross-cutting and demand evidence for hospital analytics questions

Scope: US hospitals and health systems, as of 2026-11-16. Research ran under a budget of about 17 tool calls. Many sources are trade press or vendor pages; each is flagged. Paywalled sources (KLAS full reports, Press Ganey benchmarks, Scottsdale Institute member reports) were not read. Items marked **[unverified]** come from background knowledge and were not confirmed this session.

## What do hospital boards and C-suites track on their standard dashboards, according to published guidance (AHA trustee resources, IHI board guidance, NQF)?

### Takeaway
Published guidance agrees on structure more than on content. Use a small dashboard of about 10 to 25 measures, reviewed at least quarterly, with quality and safety first, plus a few finance, patient experience, workforce and population health measures. No authoritative body publishes a standard metric list, so every system builds its own scorecard, and governance commentators criticise that. The guidance is mostly 2010 to 2019 and has not been refreshed much since.

### Cited Findings
- AHA Trustee Services best-practice checklist: use a limited number of key measures (about 10 to 25 at most), get the dashboard to the board at least quarterly, have the board help pick the measures, and have directors probe the root causes of trends and variances. — [AHA Trustee Services checklist (PDF)](https://trustees.aha.org/sites/default/files/trustees/Best_practices_dashboards.PDF); [AHA Dashboards and Balanced Scorecards](https://trustees.aha.org/dashboards-and-balanced-scorecards)
- Board dashboards usually have 3 or 4 categories: quality and patient safety, finance, population health, and culture or workforce. One governance expert says boards should lead with clinical measures and that 2 or 3 financial measures are enough, with operating margin the most common. — [AHA Trustee Services, "Board Dashboards Deliver Vital Stats"](https://trustees.aha.org/node/140220) (summarised in search results; not fetched in full)
- NRC Health/Governance Institute: boards should see only high-level measures such as risk-adjusted mortality, healthcare-associated infections (HAIs) and patient experience (would-recommend, net promoter score). Directors should track leading indicators as well as lagging results. Custom scorecards may weaken oversight, and widely accepted indicators are preferred. — [NRC Health, "Evaluating Board and Committee Dashboards" (Dec 2019)](https://nrchealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SysFcs_Evaluating-Board-and-Committee-Dashboards_Dec2019_Krein.pdf)
- Balanced scorecard content: quality, safety and patient experience; financial performance; employee engagement; turnover. — [AHA Trustee Services](https://trustees.aha.org/node/128443)
- CFO dashboard items: admissions and readmissions, community benefit (to preserve tax exemption at nonprofits), and covered lives and premium revenue where the system runs a health plan. — [Becker's, "The ultimate CFO dashboard"](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/the-ultimate-cfo-dashboard-metrics-to-watch-to-succeed-in-a-shifting-healthcare-industry.html)
- IHI Framework for Effective Board Governance of Health System Quality (2018 white paper, Daley Ullem, Gandhi, Mate et al., with the Lucian Leape Institute). It includes a Governance of Quality Assessment (GQA) tool covering 30 core quality-oversight processes, and IHI recommends CEOs use it with the board every year. IHI's research links effective governance to structure, such as having a quality committee and putting quality measures on the board dashboard. — [IHI framework page](https://ihi.org/fr/node/22816); [IHI research summary (PDF)](https://www.ihi.org/sites/default/files/IHIResearchSummary_EffectiveBoardGovernanceHealthSystemQuality.pdf); [IHI GQA tool](https://stg.ihi.org/sites/default/files/IHI_Governance-of-Quality-Assessment.pdf)
- Vendor guidance (Health Catalyst, not IHI): boards should look at "big dot" system-level measures, such as system-wide serious adverse event rates, and quality and safety should be at least half of the measures the board reviews. — [Health Catalyst](https://healthcatalyst.com/insights/healthcare-boards-quality-safety-pivotal-role)
- Boards are advised to review quality measures for all care settings at least quarterly. — Governance Institute summary cited via [IHI resources search results](https://ihi.org/resources/publications/your-board-effectively-overseeing-quality)

### Inferences
- A typical board/C-suite question set, drawn from the sources above:
  - **Quality and safety:** risk-adjusted mortality (observed/expected), HAIs (CLABSI, CAUTI, C. diff, MRSA, SSI as SIRs), serious safety events, PSI-90, readmissions, CMS star rating, Leapfrog grade.
  - **Patient experience:** HCAHPS top-box and would-recommend, NPS.
  - **Finance:** operating margin, days cash on hand, payer mix, cost per case/CMI-adjusted, length of stay and GMLOS gap, denials, AR days.
  - **Volume and growth:** admissions, ED visits, surgical cases, market share, leakage.
  - **Workforce:** turnover, vacancy, engagement, agency spend.
  - **Population health/VBC:** attributed lives, shared-savings performance, quality-gate measures.
  - **Community benefit and equity:** stratified gaps.
- The finance specifics (days cash, AR days, denials) are standard HFMA/rating-agency metrics, but no source above sets them as a board standard **[unverified as board-standard; widely used]**.
- Boards ask for interpretation as much as values: "why did this trend change" and root cause of variance are explicit AHA expectations. That points to explanation and drill-down as a core workflow, not just number retrieval.
- No standard list exists, so each system defines its own versions. The same metric name can mean different things across systems, which makes definition governance part of answering executive questions.

### Gaps
- No current (2024 to 2026) AHA or IHI board dashboard template was found. The NQF guidance asked about was not located. NQF's endorsement role moved to Battelle's Partnership for Quality Measurement in 2023 **[unverified this session]**.
- The full text of the IHI framework's third core area was not retrieved.
- No survey data was found on how many systems actually show each measure on board dashboards.

## What measures must be stratified by race, ethnicity, language, sex, payer or social risk, and by whom? (incl. CMS HCHE, Joint Commission equity standard, SDOH screening, HCAHPS, access, post-acute, telehealth)

### Takeaway
Federal hospital equity requirements shrank sharply in 2025 and 2026. The FY 2026 IPPS final rule removed the Hospital Commitment to Health Equity (HCHE) attestation, both SDOH screening measures (SDOH-1, SDOH-2) and the HVBP Health Equity Adjustment. The Joint Commission retired its NPSG chapter (which held the health care equity goal, formerly LD.04.03.08) on 2026-01-01 and folded it into 14 National Performance Goals, one of them "Excellent health outcomes for all". Stratification still required or delivered includes: confidential CMS reports stratified by dual eligibility and race/ethnicity; HRRP's dual-proportion peer grouping, which affects payment; NCQA HEDIS race/ethnicity stratification on about 22 measures, which health plans report and pass down to provider VBC contracts; and state Medicaid VBP requirements, for example New York.

### Cited Findings
**CMS hospital equity measures (IPPS)**
- The FY 2026 IPPS final rule removed the HCHE measure starting with the CY 2024 reporting period / FY 2026 payment determination. CMS cited burden exceeding benefit. The same rule removed the HVBP Health Equity Adjustment (effective FY 2026), the SDOH-1 and SDOH-2 screening measures, and the health equity plan reporting requirement. Leapfrog opposed the removals. — [Coronis Health](https://www.coronishealth.com/blog/2026-ipps-final-rule-the-final-word-on-quality); [RISE Health](https://www.risehealth.org/insights-articles/cms-issues-final-rule-for-inpatient-long-term-care-hospitals-1/); [Leapfrog comments](https://www.leapfroggroup.org/node/4586); [McDermott+ FY2026 highlights](https://www.mcdermottplus.com/?p=158040); [NRHA FY2026 IPPS final rule summary (PDF)](https://www.ruralhealth.us/NationalRuralHealth/media/Documents/Advocacy/2025/FY-2026-IPPS-final-rule-summary.pdf); [ICD10monitor, "The Undoing of SDoH Reporting"](https://icd10monitor.medlearn.com/the-undoing-of-sdoh-reporting)
- FY 2027 IPPS final rule: released 2026-07-31, published 2026-08-04 (91 FR 49570), with a 2.3% rate update. The IQR program reportedly removed 3 measures, adopted 8 and modified 3. Adopted measures include excess days in acute care after hospitalisation for diabetes, an advance care planning eCQM and a postoperative VTE eCQM. Summaries differ on details, and the full list of removed measures was not confirmed. — [ACDIS](https://acdis.org/node/70470); [ACC](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Articles/2026/08/05/19/51/CMS-Releases); [AAMC](https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/cms-releases-fy27-inpatient-prospective-payment-system-final-rule); [ECG Management Consultants](https://www.ecgmc.com/insights/blog/cms-issues-fy-2027-hospital-ipps-and-ltch-pps-final-rule); [Applied Policy, FY2027 proposed](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/?p=10861); [QualityReportingCenter IQR listserv (PDF)](https://www.qualityreportingcenter.com/globalassets/listserves/2026/iqr/2026-66-ip_iqr_ipps-final-rule-listserv_vfinal-508.pdf)

**CMS stratified confidential reporting and payer-based stratification**
- CMS sends hospitals confidential Hospital-Specific Reports (HSRs) every year. It confidentially reports condition-specific and hospital-wide readmission results stratified by insurance type (dual eligibility) and by race and ethnicity, using its Disparity Methods (within-hospital and across-hospital comparisons). These results are not public and do not affect payment. — [CMS HRRP page](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/prospective-payment-systems/acute-inpatient-pps/hospital-readmissions-reduction-program-hrrp); [QualityReportingCenter HSR materials](https://www.qualityreportingcenter.com/globalassets/migrated-pdf/iqr_transcript_hrrp-hsrs_03292018_vfinal508.pdf)
- HRRP payment uses a stratified method. Hospitals are grouped into peer groups by their share of dual-eligible patients, and each hospital's excess readmission ratio is compared with its peer-group median. This is the one payment program where social-risk stratification currently changes dollars. — [HHS HRRP Stratified Methodology user guide (PDF)](https://hhs.gov/guidance/sites/default/files/hhs-guidance-documents/HRRP_StratMethod_ImpctFile_UG.PDF)
- Inpatient psychiatric facilities: CMS issued confidential IPF readmission reports stratified by dual eligibility (FY2023 notice); they are not publicly reported. — [QualityReportingCenter IPF notice (PDF)](https://w.qualityreportingcenter.com/globalassets/listserves/2023/ipf/2023-11-ipf_fy2023-stratified-ipf-readmission-isr_final-508.pdf)

**Joint Commission**
- The health care equity standard LD.04.03.08 was elevated to NPSG.16.01.01 on 2023-07-01 with its requirements unchanged. Requirements: name an equity leader, assess patients' health-related social needs (HRSN), stratify quality and safety data to identify disparities, write an action plan, and act when goals are missed. Applies to hospitals, CAHs, some ambulatory and behavioural health organisations. — [Joint Commission news release (2023)](https://jointcommission.org/resources/news-and-multimedia/news/2023/01/the-joint-commission-elevates-health-care-equity-standard-to-national-patient-safety-goal); [Joint Commission R3 Report, July 2022 (PDF)](https://www.jointcommission.org/-/media/tjc/documents/standards/r3-reports/r3_disparities_july2022_final.pdf)
- The NPSG chapter was retired on 2026-01-01 and replaced by 14 National Performance Goals for hospitals. Goal 4 is "Excellent health outcomes for all". 714 hospital requirements were removed in the "Accreditation 360" overhaul. Secondary sources say existing requirements were reorganised rather than new ones added. Whether every equity element of performance survived (especially HRSN screening and stratification) was not confirmed from Joint Commission text. — [McDermott Will & Schulte](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/joint-commission-announces-key-accreditation-updates-for-2026/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/joint-commission-cutting-over-700-hospital-standards-accreditation-overhaul); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/joint-commission-cuts-standards-by-50-in-sweeping-overhaul)
- One vendor blog says 2026 hospitals must still use stratified data to inform equity initiatives. That is a vendor interpretation, not official text. — [Quality Interactions](https://www.qualityinteractions.com/blog/what-healthcare-teams-need-to-do-now-to-meet-2026-care-standards)

**NCQA / health plan and state Medicaid stratification (flows to health-system VBC contracts)**
- HEDIS race and ethnicity stratification (RES) now follows OMB SPD 15 (March 2024). MY 2026 requires the Middle Eastern or North African (MENA) category and renames "Some Other Race" to "Other Race". NCQA says 22 HEDIS measures can be stratified as of MY 2026, while an earlier NCQA proposal cited 23 as of MY 2025. The counts conflict. MY 2027 updates refine how declined and unknown race/ethnicity are captured. — [NCQA Data, Measurement and Equity](https://ncqa.org/health-equity/data-and-measurement); [NCQA RES updates (PDF)](https://wpcdn.ncqa.org/www-prod/09.-RES-Updates.pdf); [NCQA HEDIS MY 2027 blog](https://www.ncqa.org/blog/hedis-my-2027-whats-new-whats-changed-whats-retired/); [Cotiviti](https://resources.cotiviti.com/cotiviti-blog/ncqa-releases-proposed-hedis-my-2026-updates)
- New York Medicaid VBP (2026) requires at least one NCQA RES measure in every Level 1+ VBP contract, with results stratified per NCQA guidance. This passes stratification down to provider organisations. — [NY DOH TCGP 2026 quality measures](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/medicaid/redesign/vbp/quality_measures/2026/tcgp_qms.htm)

**HCAHPS / patient experience**
- The updated HCAHPS survey applies to discharges from 2025-01-01. It grew from 29 to 32 items and added three measures: Care Coordination, Restfulness of the Hospital Environment, and Information about Symptoms. The Care Transition measure and the call-button and ED-admission items were removed. Public reporting of the new measures starts October 2026 with CY2025 data. Sources conflict on when they enter HVBP: "CY2028 performance period" per PRC versus "FY 2030 program year" per the CMS proposed rule text. These may be the same timeline (CY2028 performance feeds FY2030 payment). — [FHA summary of CMS proposed rule (PDF)](https://www.fha.org/common/Uploaded%20files/FHA/Committees_Councils/Quality%20Patient%20Safety/IPPS%20PR%202025%20HCAHPS.pdf); [PRC (PDF)](https://prcexcellence.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/Patient_CAHPS/FINAL-HCAHPS-survey-changes-for-January-2025-002.pdf); [NRC Health sub-measures (PDF)](https://nrchealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2025-Updated-HCAHPS-Suvey-Sub-Measures.pdf); [HCAHPS Online What's New](https://ww.hcahpsonline.org/en/whats-new)
- A 12-item cap now applies to supplemental questions. — [AHRQ CAHPS Hospital Survey](https://www.ahrq.gov/cahps/surveys-guidance/hospital/about/adult_hp_survey.html)

**Access**
- Third next available appointment (TNAA): the average number of days from a patient's request to the third available slot for a new-patient physical, routine exam or return visit. The third slot is used because the first two often reflect last-minute cancellations. IHI-originated (Murray & Berwick 2003). There is no national standard for a good value, and critics say it was never validated for primary care. Cincinnati Children's and Texas Children's report it as a system-level measure. — [NAM 2015 "Getting to Now"](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/20220/chapter/4); [NAM perspectives](https://nam.edu/perspectives-2015-innovation-and-best-practices-in-health-care-scheduling/); [Cincinnati Children's](https://cincinnatichildrens.org/about/quality-measures/system-level-measures/3rd-next-available-appointment); [Texas Children's](https://www.texaschildrens.org/patients-families/safety-and-outcomes/number-clinics-with-3rd-next-available-appointment-less-than); [PAC commentary (PDF)](https://assets-002.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/pac/PDFs-and-Documents/About_Us/CommentaryonTNAA-db77ded8.pdf)
- Referral leakage: in a Fibroblast/Sage Growth Partners survey of about 100 executives (circa 2018), 43% said they lose more than 10% of revenue to leakage, 19% said more than 20%, and 23% did not know how much they lose. Most tracked leakage through the EHR and were dissatisfied with it. — [Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/losing-patients-other-providers-can-cost-some-healthcare-facilities-10-percent-their-revenue); [RevCycleIntelligence](https://revcycleintelligence.com/news/patient-leakage-costs-most-healthcare-facilities-10-of-revenue)
- Vendor analysis (Luna, 2022): 60% of health-system PT referrals among 3.9M commercially insured patients went out of network. Vendor data, not peer-reviewed. — [Luna](https://www.getluna.com/blog/orthopedic-patient-leakage)

### Inferences
- The engineering point for the catalogue: since the HCHE, SDOH-1/2 and HVBP equity adjustment were removed, equity questions in health systems are now driven by contracts (HEDIS RES in plan and Medicaid VBP contracts), accreditation (the Joint Commission performance goal), confidential CMS HSRs and voluntary or mission goals, rather than by IQR reporting. Expect "show me measure X by race/ethnicity/language/payer/dual/ADI/zip" requests to persist, but with less uniform definitions.
- Stratification dimensions seen in practice: race and ethnicity (OMB SPD 15 including MENA), preferred language, sex, payer/dual eligibility, and area deprivation or social risk. Sexual orientation/gender identity and disability show up in some state and plan requirements **[unverified this session]**.
- Many SDOH screening questions in health systems still exist for Joint Commission, plan contracts and internal programmes, even though the CMS SDOH-1/2 hospital measures are gone. Whether CMS also removed the OQR/ambulatory SDOH screening measures or the MIPS screening measure was not checked **[gap]**.
- Cross-cutting question inventory (definitions to verify against source specs):
  - **Patient experience:** HCAHPS top-box by dimension (nurse/doctor communication, responsiveness, medicines, discharge info, care coordination, restfulness, symptom info, cleanliness, overall rating, recommend), with linear mean scores for HVBP; CG-CAHPS/OAS-CAHPS for ambulatory and outpatient surgery; complaints and grievances.
  - **Access:** TNAA; days to new-patient appointment; no-show rate (no-shows / scheduled); same-day cancellations; referral completion and closed-loop rate; leakage (share of referred or attributed services delivered out of network); ED boarding and left-without-being-seen.
  - **Equity:** any core measure (readmission, mortality, HAIs, HCAHPS, HEDIS gaps, no-shows, wait times) stratified by the dimensions above, with gap and disparity ratios; screening completion and positive rates; Z-code capture.
  - **Population health/VBC:** attributed lives, PMPM cost versus benchmark, shared-savings or loss projection, quality-gate measures (HEDIS/eCQMs), HCC recapture, AWV completion, ED visits and admissions per 1,000.
  - **Post-acute:** discharge-to-SNF rate, SNF length of stay, SNF 30-day readmission by facility (preferred network performance), home health start-of-care timeliness, TEAM episode spend (TEAM started 2026-01-01) **[TEAM date unverified this session]**.
  - **Telehealth:** share of visits virtual, by specialty, payer and demographic (digital divide), and conversion to in-person.

### Gaps
- Exact current Joint Commission text for equity elements of performance under National Performance Goal 4 (HRSN screening, stratification) was not retrieved. The Joint Commission's own site would settle it.
- The full FY 2027 IQR removal list and any FY 2027 changes to stratified reporting were not confirmed.
- Not checked: CMS Medicaid Adult/Child Core Set mandatory stratification (2024 rule phase-in), Medicare Advantage stratified star-rating measures (Health Equity Index / Excellent Health Outcomes for All reward), MSSP ACO health equity adjustment status in 2026, and state hospital equity reporting laws (for example California, Massachusetts).
- No authoritative benchmark was found for no-show rates, referral leakage or SNF network performance.
- No telehealth executive-metric guidance was found.

## What is known about the volume and type of ad hoc analytics requests in health systems, and the turnaround time and backlog?

### Takeaway
Public evidence is thin and mostly anecdotal. No peer-reviewed study or CHIME, HIMSS, KLAS or Scottsdale survey found here measures health-system analytics request volume, ad hoc versus standard share, or turnaround. The best concrete data point is a case study: OSU Physicians fielded about 120 ad hoc requests a month with 2 FTEs, and a rebuilt self-service environment cut custom requests by 90%. Cross-industry surveys suggest heavy ad hoc load and turnaround of 1 to 4 weeks, but they are not healthcare-specific.

### Cited Findings
- **OSU Physicians** (faculty practice plan, Ohio State Wexner Medical Center), presented at MGMA Operations Conference in April 2026:
  - Volume: peaked at about 120 ad hoc reporting requests a month, handled by 2 FTEs through a ticket system.
  - Requesters and topics: clinic managers, practice managers and physicians, asking mainly about physician productivity, quality and patient satisfaction.
  - What was built: a rebuilt analytics environment with a common front end and role-based curated filters (dean, chairs, practice and location leaders), integrating more than a dozen source systems including EHR and HR.
  - Result: custom report requests fell 90%.
  - Hardest data problem: the HR employee-ID to NPI crosswalk.
  - Definition problems: "patient" versus "encounter", appointment versus billing encounter, and distinct patients versus visit volume.
  - Not reported: turnaround times.
  - Source: [Physicians Practice, 2026-04-01](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/how-one-health-system-turned-data-chaos-into-a-90-drop-in-report-requests)
- Health Catalyst/MultiCare: a dedicated analytics team supports "hundreds, if not thousands, of reports", with a growing backlog of encounter-related report requests. No quantification. — [Health Catalyst](https://www.healthcatalyst.com/learn/insights/advanced-data-analytics-break-free-reporting-backlog)
- Sigma Computing survey (cross-industry, not healthcare): 76% of data experts spend up to half their time on ad hoc reporting, 53% get 3 to 4 follow-up questions per request, and 55% say a request takes 1 to 4 weeks. Primary survey not fetched. — cited via [search summary; original Sigma survey not verified]
- Data request processes at academic medical centres usually need several rounds between requester and analyst to define requirements (Weill Cornell). The RI-MUHC data request centre measures turnaround in business days and separates active time from time on hold. — [Weill Cornell ITS](https://its.weill.cornell.edu/services/research-informatics/ehr-data-and-reporting); [RI-MUHC (PDF)](https://cdn.fourwaves.com/static/media/formdata/e711cf66-1c43-4144-8797-d63ff8c21fdb/0f6ec947-67de-47b7-9502-74793c955350.pdf) (RI-MUHC is Canadian, outside the US scope)
- HIMSS/Arcadia survey: less than 60% of health systems' data is used to inform business decisions. — [TechTarget HealthTech Analytics](https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechanalytics/news/366590225/Data-Utilization-Investment-Lagging-Among-Hospitals-and-Health-Systems)
- Navicure survey of more than 600 healthcare leaders: the biggest analytics challenge was lack of time and resources to use data analytics effectively (38%). — [via search; HIT Consultant/InformationWeek, date uncertain (likely 2016)](https://hitconsultant.net/2016/08/01/state-healthcare-data-analytics-reporting)
- Health executives are not using data daily to make decisions (survey headline). — [Chief Healthcare Executive](https://www.chiefhealthcareexecutive.com/view/health-execs-not-leveraging-data-daily-to-make-decisions) (not fetched; figures unverified)
- MedStar case study: limited data-team bandwidth, no real-time reporting, and a proliferation of ad hoc reports slowed responses. No turnaround figure. — [MedStar (PDF)](https://www.medstarhealth.org/-/media/project/mho/medstar/pdf/performance-improvement-analytics/analytic-case-study-ct.pdf)
- The often-cited claim that analysts spend 80% or more of their time gathering data comes from a slide deck quoting conversations, not a study. — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/getting-the-most-out-of-your-data-analyst/40274781)
- CHIME/KLAS Digital Health Most Wired 2025 names embedding analytics in workflows, connecting data across teams, and data governance as priorities. It does not measure request queues. Full report partly paywalled. — [KLAS Most Wired 2025](https://klasresearch.com/report/digital-health-most-wired-national-trends-2025/3946); [HIT Consultant summary](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/11/13/chime-klas-the-digital-health-most-wired-national-trends-report-2025/)
- Scottsdale Institute/Health Catalyst Data & Analytics report: interviews with 22 member organisations on how analytics functions are structured. Older, with no demand counts in the public summary. — [Scottsdale Institute](https://scottsdaleinstitute.org/si-research-report-data-analytics-overcoming-strategic-challenges-to-growth/)
- 2014 eHI/CHIME analytics survey exists (historical baseline). — [eHI/CHIME (PDF)](https://www.ehidc.org/sites/default/files/resources/files/2014-05-16_eHI_CHIME-Analytics-Survey_Findings_0.pdf) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- At OSU, a 90% drop after role-based self-service suggests most ad hoc requests were repeats or variants of standard questions (productivity, quality, satisfaction by provider or location) rather than new analyses. That is one case study and should be treated as directional.
- The most common request types in the sources are operational slices: provider productivity, quality by unit or provider, patient experience, encounter volumes, and filtered or regrouped versions of existing reports.
- Turnaround in health systems is likely days to weeks, driven by requirement clarification loops (Weill Cornell) and data integration. No healthcare-specific measurement exists in public sources.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of health-system analytics request queues (volume, mix, turnaround) was found. Targeted PubMed/JAMIA/eGEMs searches were not done within budget.
- KLAS reports on analytics platforms and the full Most Wired data are paywalled and were not read.
- The share of ad hoc versus standard reports has no healthcare-specific figure.
- The primary Sigma survey was not located, so its figures are unverified.

## Which questions are hardest to answer today, and why (data silos, definitions, chart abstraction)?

### Takeaway
The sources name three recurring obstacles: (1) identity and entity crosswalks across systems (HR vs EHR vs billing; provider ID vs NPI); (2) definition ambiguity ("patient", "encounter", "visit", "admission") and unstandardised scorecards; (3) manual chart abstraction for quality measures. Equity stratification adds missing or declined race, ethnicity and language data. Leakage and post-acute questions need data from outside the system (claims), which the EHR does not hold.

### Cited Findings
- Physician data was the hardest integration: HR uses an employee ID and the EHR uses the NPI, and the crosswalk was inconsistent. This also affected APPs and registration and rooming staff. Joining HR to the EHR made labour hours per encounter possible and showed different staffing for the same specialty across sites. — [Physicians Practice](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/how-one-health-system-turned-data-chaos-into-a-90-drop-in-report-requests)
- Definition ambiguity: "patients" and "encounters" mean different things (appointment vs billing encounter; distinct patients vs visit volume). Advice: document shared definitions early. — [Physicians Practice](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/how-one-health-system-turned-data-chaos-into-a-90-drop-in-report-requests)
- Data scattered across EMR, billing and costing systems that are not integrated. — [SlideShare (informal)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/getting-the-most-out-of-your-data-analyst/40274781)
- Clinical data abstractors spend more than half their time on manual entry and extraction (Carta Healthcare survey, 2024; vendor-sponsored). — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2024/08/13/clinical-data-abstractors-overburdened-by-manual-tasks)
- Leakage: most organisations track it in the EHR and are dissatisfied with that, and 23% do not know their leakage. — [Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/losing-patients-other-providers-can-cost-some-healthcare-facilities-10-percent-their-revenue)
- Hospitals told CMS that SDOH screening, data storage, staff training and workflow changes were costly with little added value. That burden is part of why CMS removed the measures. — [Coronis Health](https://www.coronishealth.com/blog/2026-ipps-final-rule-the-final-word-on-quality)
- NCQA's MY 2027 changes explicitly address capturing declined and unknown race/ethnicity, which shows missing demographic data is a known stratification problem. — [NCQA HEDIS MY 2027](https://www.ncqa.org/blog/hedis-my-2027-whats-new-whats-changed-whats-retired/)
- TNAA has no national standard and contested validity, so cross-site access comparisons depend on local definitions. — [PAC commentary (PDF)](https://assets-002.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/pac/PDFs-and-Documents/About_Us/CommentaryonTNAA-db77ded8.pdf)
- Governance commentary says custom board scorecards may reduce oversight effectiveness, which is a definition problem at board level. — [NRC Health (PDF)](https://nrchealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SysFcs_Evaluating-Board-and-Committee-Dashboards_Dec2019_Krein.pdf)

### Inferences
- Hardest question classes, ranked by the obstacles above:
  1. **Leakage and total cost of care:** need claims outside the system.
  2. **Post-acute and SNF network performance:** need outside facility outcomes and claims.
  3. **Stratified equity gaps:** missing race, ethnicity and language data, small cells, changing category standards (SPD 15/MENA).
  4. **Abstracted quality measures** (sepsis SEP-1, PC-01 and similar): need chart abstraction.
  5. **Productivity and staffing per encounter:** HR/EHR crosswalk.
  6. **"Why did X change":** needs a consistent definition plus attribution or decomposition.
- For an analytics product: governed definitions, entity crosswalks and stated stratification denominators (including unknown/declined as an explicit group) directly address the documented obstacles. Supported "unknown" answers, for example when outside claims are absent, are legitimate outcomes.
