# Routing, cascades, calibration and abstention: arXiv 2025 to November 2026

Read-depth tags used on every finding. **[FULL]** = arXiv HTML read (long pages were read to roughly the first 100,000 characters, so appendices may be missing). **[ABS]** = abstract page only. **[SNIP]** = search-result snippet only, not opened; treat as a lead, not a finding. All results are the authors' own, in the scope stated. Nothing here was reproduced. "Test here" lines name the baseline already in place.

System shorthand: R1 saved recipes, R2 wording-matched recipes, R3 System One typed classifier (fixed 0.8 cut-off), R4 small LLM, R5 frontier SQL, R6 bounded frontier agent. Weak spots: (a) method routing 19/28, (b) R6 cost 3 to 25 cents and 17 to 55 s, (c) one global threshold, (d) no trained router despite hundreds of logged outcomes.

## 1. LLM routing and cascades: learned routers, cost-aware deferral, fitted thresholds, bandits, escalation policies; data volume and transfer failures

### Takeaway
The strongest 2025 to 2026 evidence says a nearest-neighbour router over logged outcomes is as good as trained routers on public benchmarks, and that more data, bigger encoders and fine-tuning add about one point; the remaining gap to an oracle sits in hard queries that query text alone does not separate. Thresholds fitted per stage from logged outcomes (isotonic or Platt calibration, then a cost-minimising cut-off under an accuracy target) beat hand-set and raw-score thresholds in the one careful head-to-head found, but every reported gain is in-distribution and abstention heads in particular collapse under shift.

### Cited Findings

**1.1 kNN router as the baseline to beat**
- *Rethinking Predictive Modeling for LLM Routing: When Simple kNN Beats Complex Learned Routers*, arXiv 2505.12601 (Yang Li, 19 May 2025) **[FULL]**. Method: embed query (BERT-base CLS, L2-normalised), take k nearest logged queries (k = 10 or 100) by cosine, average their observed score and cost, or majority-vote their best model. Benchmarks: AlpacaEval, Open LLM Leaderboard v2, HELM-Lite, RouterBench (11 models), plus five vHELM vision sets; random 70/10/20 splits; absolute training sizes not reported. — [arXiv 2505.12601](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12601v1)
- Author results, text utility prediction (average AUC): kNN k=100 52.68, Linear 53.14, MLP-MF 52.88, Graph 51.82, MLP 51.71, oracle 64.91, random 40.35. Text model selection (average utility): kNN k=10 39.75, Linear 42.33, MLP 42.85, Double-Attentive 43.31. So kNN ties on utility prediction and trails by about 3 points on direct selection; the title claim holds only in part. — [arXiv 2505.12601](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12601v1)
- Limitation: no out-of-distribution or cross-task test; the sample-complexity theorem (kNN needs fewer samples when performance is locally smooth in embedding space) has no controlled sample-size experiment behind it. — [arXiv 2505.12601](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12601v1)
- **Test here:** kNN over logged question to recipe/method outcomes using the installed local encoder (k in 5 to 20, vote weighted by logged success) versus current R2 wording match plus R3 classifier, scored on the 28 held-out method questions and by leave-one-out on the ledger.

**1.2 The routing plateau: more data and bigger routers do not help much**
- *The Routing Plateau: Understanding the Accuracy Limits of LLM Routers*, arXiv 2606.07587 (Lu et al., 27 May 2026, v2 28 Sep 2026) **[FULL, first 100k chars]**. 21 routing methods, 5 benchmarks (RouterBench, CARROT/SPROUT, R2-Bench, Nine-by-30k, EmbedLLM). — [arXiv 2606.07587](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07587)
- Author results: top-5 routers differ by 0.04 to 0.52 points of test accuracy per benchmark (mean 0.22); best router trails the oracle by 10.6 to 29.4 points; kNN is in the top two on all five; learned routers gain at most 0.006 concordance over kNN (for example RouterBench kNN 0.768 versus 0.762 to 0.772 for 11 others). — [arXiv 2606.07587](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07587)
- Data scaling: growing training queries from 23,027 to 302,371 with a frozen encoder added 0.47 points on average across eight routers; fine-tuning on the small set added 0.42; both together 1.19; plus a larger encoder 1.24 (range 0.31 to 2.13). Mean accuracy stayed 13.27 points under the oracle. — [arXiv 2606.07587](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07587)
- "Hard" queries (only a few models answer correctly) are 8.5% to 43.2% of each benchmark but 53.3% to 80.7% of the oracle gap. Authors' hypothesis (not tested by them): routers learn average model strength, not instance-level correctness, and progress needs signals beyond the query, such as a partial attempt. — [arXiv 2606.07587](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07587)
- **Test here:** before building any trained router, check whether kNN on the ledger already equals a small trained classifier (logistic regression on encoder embeddings) on the same split; if the two agree within noise, stop and spend effort on post-attempt signals (section 2 and 3) instead.

**1.3 Fair evaluation and out-of-distribution behaviour**
- *Towards Fair and Comprehensive Evaluation of Routers in Collaborative LLM Systems* (RouterXBench), arXiv 2602.11877 (Wu et al., 12 Feb 2026) **[FULL]**. A linear probe on the small model's cross-layer hidden states reached average AUROC 68.70 in-domain and 65.46 out-of-domain, versus 59.46 and 55.22 for the best external-embedding baseline (EmbeddingMLP); other baselines sat near 49 to 51 out-of-domain, which is chance. Trained on 12K examples (4K each from three sets); 1K per dataset was insufficient, 4K to 8K gave minimal gain. — [arXiv 2602.11877](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11877)
- Negative findings from the same paper: adding MLP layers gave nothing and overfit; final-layer-only probing was poor (AUROC 53.91); routing cannot help when both models share the same wrong answer. Limitations: one model pair (Llama-3.1-8B and GPT-5), single seed, GPT-5 as judge for open-ended labels. — [arXiv 2602.11877](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11877)
- *Beyond Query Memorization* (DecoR, CodaSet), arXiv 2605.25558 (Lv and Sun, 25 May 2026, ACL 2026) **[ABS]**. Claims surface-feature routers fall into a "memorization trap" and generalise poorly out of distribution; proposes splitting a query into capability requirements before matching against historical logs. No numbers on the abstract page. A third-party summary says prior routers fail to beat random selection on out-of-distribution tasks **[SNIP, unverified]**. — [arXiv 2605.25558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25558)
- **Test here:** split the ledger by time (train on earlier weeks, test on the latest) and by topic family as well as at random; report all three. A router that only wins on the random split is memorising wording, which R1/R2 already do for free.

**1.4 Trained router with abstention for tool calling (closest analogue to this stack)**
- *Switchcraft: AI Model Router for Agentic Tool Calling*, arXiv 2605.07112 (Agarwal et al., Microsoft Research, v2 30 Oct 2026) **[FULL, first 100k chars]**. DistilBERT (67M) emits one logit per candidate model; per-model Platt scaling turns logits into correctness probabilities; a logistic-regression head estimates "no model will be right" and abstains; router picks the cheapest model above a reliability threshold. Trained on 77,891 queries (80/10/10 split of 97,369 from five function-calling sets), each run against eight LLMs; a sixth set (Glaive, 83,814) held out entirely. — [arXiv 2605.07112](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07112)
- Author results in-distribution: 78.27% answer accuracy at 27.5e-4 dollars per query versus best single model 75.63% at 66.2e-4 (about 59% cheaper); kNN k=10 76.49%; random routing 66.89%; oracle 85.71%. Router latency about 16.7 ms P99 on a T4. — [arXiv 2605.07112](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07112)
- Negative results, held-out Glaive: trained router 89.39 to 89.88% versus kNN 89.43% (the lead over retrieval disappears) and best single model 88.53%; the abstention head caught 2.8% of impossible queries out of distribution versus 56.9% in distribution. ModernBERT and DeBERTa-v3 gave no meaningful gain over DistilBERT. The router must be retrained when the model pool changes; labels are single runs. — [arXiv 2605.07112](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07112)
- **Test here:** per-rung Platt scaling on ledger outcomes plus a separate "no cheap rung will succeed, go straight to R5" head, compared with the current strict ladder order; measure how many R3/R4 attempts that later failed would have been skipped, and re-measure the abstention head on the time-split because this is the part that failed to transfer.

**1.5 Thresholds fitted from logged outcomes instead of a fixed cut-off**
- *UCCI: Calibrated Uncertainty for Cost-Optimal LLM Cascade Routing*, arXiv 2605.18796 (Kotte, 11 May 2026, independent researcher) **[FULL]**. Small-model score = one minus mean top-1/top-2 token margin; isotonic regression maps score to error probability on a calibration set; threshold chosen on a validation set as the cheapest that meets an accuracy target. Data: 75,000 labelled NER queries, 22,500 calibration / 15,000 validation / 37,500 test; 4B small model (F1 0.847), 12B large (F1 0.932, cost 3.02x by measured latency). — [arXiv 2605.18796](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18796v1)
- Author results at F1 0.91: UCCI cost 2.08, split-conformal 2.18, FrugalGPT-style learned threshold 2.24, entropy threshold 2.31, large-only 3.02 (31% saving, CI 27 to 35%). ECE 0.12 raw to 0.03 with isotonic; temperature scaling reached only 0.08. Token margin beat entropy and max-probability as the raw signal. — [arXiv 2605.18796](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18796v1)
- Limitations: one workload, one model pair; the conformal baseline used the uncalibrated margin, so the comparison mixes two changes; calibration map is static; the optimality proof assumes large-model accuracy is the same on escalated queries (measured 0.928 versus 0.932). Single author, not peer reviewed as far as the page shows. — [arXiv 2605.18796](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18796v1)
- **Test here:** fit isotonic regression per R3 decision type from ledger (probability, was the final answer accepted/verified) pairs, pick each cut-off as the cheapest meeting a stated error target, and compare escalation rate and wrong-accept rate against the global 0.8 on a held-out ledger slice.
- *RouteNLP: Closed-Loop LLM Routing with Conformal Cascading and Distillation Co-Optimization*, arXiv 2604.23577 (Guo, Wu, Yiu, 26 Apr 2026, ACL 2026 Industry) **[ABS]**. Conformal prediction initialises per-tier escalation thresholds; escalation failures are clustered and used for targeted distillation into the cheaper tier, then the router is retrained. Author results: 8-week pilot at about 5K queries/day, 58% inference cost reduction, 91% response acceptance, p99 latency 1,847 ms to 387 ms; six-task benchmark 40 to 85% cost reduction at 96 to 100% quality retention on structured tasks; targeted distillation gave "over twice" the cost improvement of untargeted. "Acceptance" and quality metrics are not defined in the abstract; no limitations visible. — [arXiv 2604.23577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23577)
- **Test here:** cluster ledger rows that fell to R5/R6, rank clusters by total spend, and write a recipe or method binding for the top cluster; compare last-rung traffic share before and after against simply adding recipes in arrival order.
- *C3PO: Optimized LLM Cascades with Probabilistic Cost Constraints for Reasoning*, arXiv 2511.07396 (Valkanas et al., 10 Nov 2025, NeurIPS 2025 per search listing) **[ABS]**. Label-free: builds the cascade from unlabelled model outputs, minimising regret against the strongest model, and uses conformal prediction to bound the probability that per-query cost exceeds a budget. Claims state of the art on GSM8K, MATH-500, BigBench-Hard, AIME and effectiveness "even with small calibration sets"; no sizes or numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2511.07396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07396)
- **Test here:** not a first candidate; relevant only as a cost cap on R6 (bound the chance a question exceeds, say, 10 cents) checked against the ledger's empirical cost distribution.

**1.6 Bandit and online routing**
- *Learning to Route LLMs from Bandit Feedback: One Policy, Many Trade-offs* (BaRP), arXiv 2510.07429 (Wei et al., 8 Oct 2025) **[ABS]**. Trains under the deployment restriction that only the chosen model's outcome is seen; contextual bandit over prompt features plus a preference vector that changes the cost/quality trade-off without retraining. Author claims: beats strong offline routers by at least 12.46% and the largest LLM by at least 2.45%; metric and data sizes not on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2510.07429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07429)
- *OrcaRouter*, arXiv 2605.30736 (May 2026, technical report) **[SNIP]**. LinUCB contextual bandit; warm start on prompts where every arm's reward is known, then per-request updates on the chosen arm only; reports second place on the public RouterArena leaderboard. — [arXiv 2605.30736](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30736)
- *Contextual Queueing Bandits*, arXiv 2602.02061 (Feb 2026) **[SNIP]**. Uses user retries as implicit negative feedback and handles routing and scheduling jointly. — [arXiv 2602.02061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02061)
- **Test here:** the ledger is bandit feedback already (only the rung that ran has an outcome). Offline first: replay the ledger with inverse-propensity weighting to estimate what a kNN or LinUCB policy would have cost; only then consider a small exploration rate on live traffic. Baseline is the fixed ladder order.

### Inferences
- With hundreds, not tens of thousands, of logged outcomes, the evidence points to kNN or a linear model on frozen embeddings, not a fine-tuned router. Every trained router above used 12K to 300K labelled queries, and the two that reported sample sensitivity found under about 1K per domain insufficient (2602.11877) and 13x more data worth under half a point (2606.07587).
- The encoder and reranker "showed no gain on one small test". That test and the kNN-router idea are different uses: the papers use the encoder to look up outcomes of similar past questions, not to rank passages. A null on one does not predict the other.
- The plateau result implies the biggest remaining routing gain here is unlikely to come from a better pre-attempt router and more likely from cheap post-attempt checks (did the SQL run, did the method's required inputs bind, does a second judgment agree), which is what a cascade already is.
- This stack differs from the benchmarks in a way that favours it: benchmark routers choose among general LLMs with similar abilities, while R1/R2/methods are narrow and either apply or do not. Applicability is a more learnable target than "which LLM will be right", so benchmark ceilings are probably pessimistic here. This is an inference with no direct paper support.

### Gaps
- No paper found that trains or evaluates a router on a few hundred logged outcomes; the smallest reported effective size is about 4K per domain. Whether kNN works at 300 to 800 rows is untested in the literature read.
- No paper found applying off-policy evaluation (inverse propensity, doubly robust) to LLM routing logs; the bandit papers were abstract or snippet reads only.
- BaRP, OrcaRouter, DecoR, C3PO and RouteNLP were not read in full; their numbers and limitations need a full read before any design decision rests on them.
- RouterBench and RouteLLM themselves (2024) were not re-read; they appear here only as benchmarks used by the 2025 to 2026 papers.

## 2. Calibrating confidence for classification-style judgments and generated answers: conformal methods, scaling, selective prediction, verbalised versus logit confidence; guarantees and assumptions

### Takeaway
Conformal methods give a finite-sample, distribution-free guarantee, but only on average over exchangeable data and only relative to the labels used for calibration; a September 2026 text-to-SQL study found certificates calibrated at a nominal 10% risk carried 17 to 20 points of risk under blinded expert labels. For a typed classifier that returns a probability, per-task isotonic or Platt calibration fitted on logged outcomes is the cheapest reliable upgrade, and token or classifier probabilities track correctness better than a model's stated confidence.

### Cited Findings

**2.1 Conformal acceptance rule for a cascade tier**
- *Conformal Cascade: Distribution-Free Accuracy Guarantees for Multi-Tier LLM Inference*, arXiv 2607.25018 (Dou, Lian, Li, 27 Jul 2026, v3 31 Jul 2026) **[ABS]**. A tier accepts when its calibrated prediction set contains exactly one answer and defers otherwise; needs only black-box access and no training. Guarantee: accepting tier's set covers the true answer with probability at least 1 − Kα across K tiers by a union bound, tightening to 1 − α under a "selection-preservation" condition the authors say is consistent with but not implied by their results. Evaluated on 18 multiple-choice benchmarks with two-tier cascades from four open-weight families; "strictly improves over the strongest calibration-tuned heuristic cascade on most family–benchmark pairs". No numbers or calibration sizes on the abstract page. Limitation stated: multiple choice only; open-ended answers need an answer-clustering step left to future work. — [arXiv 2607.25018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25018)
- **Test here:** for R3 fixed-option judgments, build a split-conformal set from System One's per-option probabilities on logged labelled decisions; accept only singleton sets; compare accepted-and-wrong rate and escalation rate with the 0.8 cut-off on held-out ledger rows.
- *Prune 'n Predict*, arXiv 2501.00555 (Jan 2025) **[SNIP]**. Uses conformal set size as the deferral signal; lower size cut-offs raise accuracy on retained answers at the cost of more deferrals. — [arXiv 2501.00555](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00555)

**2.2 Conformal risk control for a routing threshold between a primary and a guardian**
- *Conformal Arbitrage: Risk-Controlled Balancing of Competing Objectives in Language Models*, arXiv 2506.00911 (Overman and Bayati, Stanford, 1 Jun 2025, NeurIPS 2025) **[SNIP plus listing]**. Post-hoc threshold deciding when a primary model's output is used and when a conservative guardian (another model or a human) takes over; calibrated with conformal risk control for a finite-sample bound on the frequency of the undesirable event; API-level, no weights or logits needed. — [arXiv 2506.00911](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00911)
- **Test here:** treat "cheap rung answered and the answer was later judged wrong" as the controlled loss and fit the R3/R4 hand-off threshold by conformal risk control at a stated risk level; compare realised risk on a later ledger slice against the nominal level.

**2.3 What the conformal guarantee does not cover (negative results)**
- *Certified Against Which Oracle? Execution Labels Set the Reported Risk of Conformal Abstention for Text-to-SQL*, arXiv 2609.25938 (Liu et al., 22 Sep 2026) **[ABS]**. Preregistered intervention on Spider-Realistic with four SQL-specialist checkpoints and two split schemes: replacing the single shipped database with the multi-instance test suite raised a certificate's held-out risk by 2.73 to 10.23 points above what its own labels reported. Under blinded labels from two SQL experts, a certificate calibrated at nominal 0.10 carried 20.0 and 17.2 points of risk on two checkpoints. The stricter oracle erred in both directions. All 16 of 16 execution-consistency scores looked better under the labels of the oracle that built their clusters. Calibration sizes not on the abstract page; one benchmark. — [arXiv 2609.25938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25938)
- **Test here:** before trusting any fitted threshold on R5 SQL, audit the label. Take a sample of ledger rows labelled "succeeded" because the query executed and returned rows, hand-check them against the question, and report how many are semantically wrong; that rate is a floor under any certificate.
- *Reliable Text-to-SQL with Adaptive Abstention*, arXiv 2501.10858 (Chen, Chen, Koudas, Yu, 18 Jan 2025, SIGMOD/PACMMOD) **[FULL, first 100k chars]**. Per-layer classifiers on the generating model's hidden states flag schema-linking tokens likely to be wrong; conformal aggregation gives at least 1 − α marginal coverage under exchangeability (1 − 2α for the majority-vote aggregate). On BIRD dev at α = 0.1: table exact match 98.89% with true-abstention rate 19.10% and false-abstention rate 12.77%; columns 97.38%, 22.01%, 13.53%. Downstream execution accuracy 65.19% versus 66.21% with gold schemas. — [arXiv 2501.10858](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10858v1)
- Limitations: needs white-box hidden states at every layer and gold schema labels (not available for a hosted frontier model); assumes no drift; false abstention of 12 to 14%; only small open models (DeepSeek-7B, CodeS) tested; a surrogate filter cut false abstention to 2.2% but dropped exact match to 90.80%. — [arXiv 2501.10858](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10858v1)
- **Test here:** only applicable to the local 4B model. For hosted rungs, the transferable part is the design: abstain at schema linking (table/column choice) and ask or escalate, before SQL is written. Compare against the current behaviour of generating SQL and checking afterwards.
- *Confidence Estimation for Error Detection in Text-to-SQL Systems*, arXiv 2501.09527 (Somov and Tutubalina, Jan 2025) **[SNIP via third-party review]**. Selective classifier over an uncertainty score decides to return SQL or abstain; reported to struggle on longer and more complex queries. — [arXiv 2501.10858 search listing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10858v1)
- *Cluster Frequency Conformal Prediction for Local Coverage*, arXiv 2605.24872 (Lavi, Shapira, Rappoport, 24 May 2026) **[ABS]**. Class-conditional conformal becomes unreliable or over-conservative when each class has few calibration examples; pools calibration data through embedding clusters with shrinkage to a global prior. Keeps finite-sample marginal validity; local validity only under unstated extra assumptions. Best class coverage in 15 of 16 dataset/score comparisons; not LLM-specific. — [arXiv 2605.24872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24872)
- **Test here:** when fitting per-decision thresholds, pool sparse decision types by embedding cluster with shrinkage toward the global fit, versus (i) one global threshold and (ii) fully separate thresholds; report wrong-accept rate per decision type.

**2.4 Scaling methods per task**
- Isotonic regression reached ECE 0.03 versus 0.08 for temperature scaling and 0.12 raw on a 4B model's token-margin signal, with 22,500 calibration examples (UCCI, scope as in 1.5). — [arXiv 2605.18796](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18796v1)
- Per-model Platt scaling plus a jointly tuned abstention threshold added accuracy in Switchcraft's ablation; the abstention calibration did not survive a dataset shift (56.9% to 2.8% recall of impossible queries). — [arXiv 2605.07112](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07112)

**2.5 Verbalised versus probability-based confidence**
- *Reported Confidence in LLMs Tracks Commitment More Than Correctness*, arXiv 2606.29490 (Kumaran, 28 Jun 2026) **[ABS]**. Two-stage setup across four non-reasoning and four reasoning models and four benchmarks: stated confidence predicted the model's own commit/abstain decision substantially better than it predicted correctness, while calibrated token log-probabilities tracked correctness. No numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2606.29490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29490)
- arXiv 2510.20460 (Oct 2025) **[SNIP]**: compares verbalised confidence, maximum softmax probability, sample consistency and a hybrid (CoCoA); reports verbalised confidence is systematically overestimated, most in instruction-tuned models. — [arXiv 2510.20460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20460v1)
- Conflicting evidence: arXiv 2603.22161 **[SNIP]** reports that verbal confidence after isotonic calibration still predicted abstention independently of log-probability confidence; arXiv 2604.05306 **[SNIP]** argues that models trained to state uncertainty give a better selective-prediction signal than simple calibration baselines. Neither was opened. — [arXiv 2603.22161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22161); [arXiv 2604.05306](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05306v1)
- *Uncertainty Quantification for LLM Agents: A Taxonomy, an Evaluation Protocol, and an Empirical Study*, arXiv 2609.07395 (Lin et al., 7 Sep 2026) **[ABS]**. On real agent traces from four models (up to 50 steps): step errors are coupled along a trajectory, step-level calibration does not imply trajectory-level calibration, confidence from the agent's own responses "does not consistently beat a simple baseline", and averaging hides late-stage overconfidence. No numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2609.07395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07395)
- **Test here:** where R4/R5 produce a self-rated confidence, log it next to (i) System One's probability on "does this answer the question as asked" and (ii) agreement of two independent samples; compare the three by AUROC against audited correctness. Baseline is the present rule (R6 answers marked unverified, others trusted).

### Inferences
- Standard split-conformal theory makes the guarantee marginal and its realised coverage variable at small n: with around 28 labelled method-routing examples the achievable risk levels are coarse (steps of roughly 1/29) and run-to-run coverage varies by several points. The method-routing set is too small to certify anything; the ledger's hundreds of rows are the usable calibration pool. (Textbook property of split conformal; not taken from a paper read in this pass.)
- 19 of 28 is 68% with a 95% Wilson interval of roughly 49% to 82%. Any technique tested on that set alone needs a difference of about 6 or more questions to be distinguishable from noise; enlarging the held-out set is a precondition for the tests in this file.
- The two transfer failures (Switchcraft abstention, text-to-SQL certificates) share a cause: the calibration labels or distribution differed from deployment. For this system the exposure is the same, because "query executed" is the cheap label and "answer was right" is the wanted one.
- Since System One returns a probability over fixed options, it is already in the regime where isotonic or Platt calibration and singleton-set conformal acceptance apply directly; no method read requires logits from the frontier model.

### Gaps
- No paper found that calibrates a typed-judgment classifier of the System One kind with fewer than about 1,000 labelled decisions per task; minimum workable calibration size for per-decision thresholds is unanswered.
- Calibration set sizes for Conformal Cascade and the text-to-SQL certificate study are not on the abstract pages.
- No numbers obtained for the verbalised-versus-logit comparison; the direction of the finding rests on one abstract and one snippet, and two snippets point the other way.
- No dedicated 2025 to 2026 survey of conformal prediction for LLMs was located in this pass.

## 3. Selecting among many intents, tools or skills, and catching "plausible but wrong tool" selections

### Takeaway
General-purpose retrievers are poor at tool retrieval without tool-specific training, and the dominant failure with larger option sets is confusion between near-duplicate or overlapping options; merging or sharpening overlapping options and a retrieve, rerank, confirm pipeline are the reported fixes. One September 2026 paper evaluates Jev, which appears to be the same family as this system's System One classifier, and reports that its reliability depends on the size of the action set and on near-valid alternatives, which matches weak spot (a).

### Cited Findings

**3.1 Typed fast decision layer in an agent (direct relevance)**
- *REFLEX with Jev for Efficient Selective Control in LLM Agents*, arXiv 2609.26532 (Wu and Lim, 22 Sep 2026) **[ABS]**. Jev acts as a "fast, typed decision layer"; a strong LLM is called when Jev's confidence is low or generation is required. Author results: 95% task success on a frozen 100-task benchmark with 72.7% fewer strong-model calls than a strong-only agent, holding across three fallback families. — [arXiv 2609.26532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26532)
- Negative and scope findings from the same abstract: reliability "depends on action-set size and on near-valid alternatives near authorization boundaries"; on external BFCL and τ-style evaluations the advantage over a cheap generative cascade was limited where ordinary routing was already highly accurate. Thresholds, model sizes and the strong model are not given on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2609.26532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26532)
- From the search snippet for the same paper **[SNIP]**: tool selection was 98.4% accurate while the decision whether to call any function at all was 52.0% accurate. — [arXiv 2609.26532 summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.26532.md)
- **Test here:** measure System One accuracy on method choice as a function of the number of options offered (all ~60, a shortlist of 5, a shortlist of 2 plus "none of these") on the 28 held-out questions and logged cases; baseline is the current single call.

**3.2 Tool retrieval quality**
- *Retrieval Models Aren't Tool-Savvy: Benchmarking Tool Retrieval for Large Language Models* (ToolRet), arXiv 2503.01763 (Shi et al., 3 Mar 2025, ACL 2025) **[ABS]**. 7.6k retrieval tasks over a 43k-tool corpus; models strong on conventional retrieval benchmarks perform poorly; a 200k-instance training set "substantially" improves retrievers. Search snippet **[SNIP]**: replacing oracle tools with retrieved tools dropped GPT-3.5-turbo pass rate on ToolBench-G1 from 62.00% to 50.60%. — [arXiv 2503.01763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01763)
- **Test here:** build retrieval text for each of the ~60 methods from its description plus logged questions that correctly used it, and measure recall@5 of the true method with the local encoder; compare with description-only text. Recall@5 is the right first metric because the confirm step can only pick from what retrieval returns.

**3.3 Overlapping options and hierarchical selection**
- *ToolScope* (Liu et al., Oracle, arXiv 2025) **[SNIP via third-party summary]**. Merges redundant or overlapping tools into canonical entries, then hybrid retrieval and reranking; reported +34.6% correct-selection rate on Seal-Tools with GPT-4o. arXiv ID not confirmed. — [summary page](https://papers.lunadong.com/paper/7414)
- *HGMF: A Hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Framework for Scalable Tool Invocation within the Model Context Protocol*, arXiv 2508.07602 (Aug 2025) **[SNIP]**. Hierarchical clustering prunes the tool set before an LLM rerank. — [arXiv 2508.07602](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07602v2)
- Hi-RAG (Tian and Zhou, Expert Systems journal, not arXiv) **[SNIP]**: coarse-to-fine service then tool retrieval on a 201-tool benchmark; Top-1 up to 7.5 points (single-service) and 10.0 (multi-service) over flat retrieval, token use down up to 89% against full-context injection. — [listing](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/hi-scpragscp-a-hierarchical-retrievalaugmented-generation-framework-for-scalable-and-generalisable-tool-selection-in-large-language-model-agents/)
- RAG-MCP (May 2025) **[SNIP, secondhand]**: retrieval before selection raised tool-selection accuracy from 13.62% to 43.13% in a large-tool stress test. Not verified against the paper. — [search summary of ToolRet-related work](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.01763)
- **Test here:** build a confusion matrix of the 9 wrong routings plus logged misroutes; where two methods are confused repeatedly, either merge them, add a discriminating clause to both descriptions, or group them under a family and select family first. Compare 28-set accuracy against the flat list.

**3.4 Catching a plausible but wrong selection**
- Switchcraft's separate abstention head ("no candidate will be right") recovered 56.9% of impossible queries in distribution and 2.8% on a held-out dataset; joint tuning of abstention with routing thresholds helped in ablation. — [arXiv 2605.07112](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07112)
- *Efficient Out-of-Scope Detection in Dialogue Systems via Uncertainty-Driven LLM Routing*, arXiv 2507.01541 (Zaera et al., 2 Jul 2025) **[ABS]**. Uncertainty estimate on an in-scope intent classifier; only high-uncertainty cases go to a fine-tuned LLM for the final in-scope/out-of-scope decision. Claims state of the art on out-of-scope benchmarks including data from a deployed system handling tens of thousands of interactions a day; no numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2507.01541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01541)
- *The Confidence Dichotomy: Analyzing and Mitigating Miscalibration in Tool-Use Agents*, arXiv 2601.07264 (Jan 2026) **[SNIP]**. Evidence tools such as web search induce severe overconfidence in agents. — [listing](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2601.07264)
- UQ-for-agents survey (2609.07395) **[SNIP]**: confidence in "should a tool be called and which" and confidence in the final answer diverge and need separate calibration and thresholds. — [arXiv 2609.07395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07395)
- **Test here:** add a second, differently framed typed judgment after method selection ("does this method's output answer the question asked: yes / no / partly", shown the method's required inputs and the bound parameters), and require both to pass. Measure on the 9 known wrong routings how many are caught and on the 19 right ones how many are wrongly rejected; baseline is the single accept at 0.8.

### Inferences
- With about 60 methods, the published evidence fits a three-step shape: encoder retrieval to a shortlist of about 5, System One choosing among the shortlist with an explicit "none of these" option, then a separate confirm judgment. Each step has a separately measurable failure rate (recall@5, shortlist accuracy, confirm precision), which the current single accept does not.
- The "plausible but wrong method accepted by the classifier" pattern is what REFLEX describes as near-valid alternatives. A high probability on the wrong option means the options are not separable from the text given, so a threshold change alone will not remove these; either the option descriptions change or a second independent signal is added.
- The 2.8% out-of-distribution abstention recall is a warning that a learned "none of these" detector should be validated on questions from outside the logged families, not only on a random hold-out.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures detection of wrong-but-plausible tool choices specifically (as distinct from out-of-scope detection or hallucinated tools); the closest evidence is one abstract-level statement in REFLEX.
- REFLEX was read at abstract level only. Its threshold choices, action-set-size curve and the identity of "Jev" with this system's classifier need confirmation from the full text; the link is inferred from the name.
- ToolScope, HGMF, RAG-MCP numbers are secondhand and unverified.
- No evidence found on example-based routing (adding logged correct questions to each option) at the scale of tens of options; the retrieval-text test in 3.2 is motivated by ToolRet's training-data finding, not by a direct result.

## 4. Distilling logged frontier decisions into small classifiers or small language models for routing and parameter extraction

### Takeaway
Two industrial papers report distilling production function-calling traffic into a small router plus a roughly 1B parameter model that fills parameters, with large latency gains, but neither reports accuracy numbers or evaluation splits in the text read, and both worked from tens of thousands of examples. The text-to-SQL distillation papers found concern SQL generation, not routing, and no negative-result paper on small-data distillation for routing was located.

### Cited Findings
- *ODIA: Oriented Distillation for Inline Acceleration of LLM-based Function Calling*, arXiv 2507.08877 (Zhang et al., ByteDance, 10 Jul 2025) **[FULL]**. Mines production function-calling records: groups identical queries, clusters by semantic similarity and by entity-slot templates, labels a cluster "simple" when its most frequent function passes a share threshold, and discards the off-dominant records. Trains (i) an intent router that decides whether the small model may take the query (options named: XGBoost, Random Forest, BERT; target above 95% accuracy under 50 ms) and (ii) a parameter model, deepseek-coder-1.3B, given the system prompt, up to three recent turns and the query (target under 300 ms). Incremental daily batches of 80,000 to 100,000 examples. — [arXiv 2507.08877](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08877v1)
- Author results: small model handled about 60% of traffic; expected latency 1,600 ms to 870 ms (45%), median 1,600 ms to 350 ms (78%); accuracy described only as "negligible degradation" with no figures; evaluation splits not stated. — [arXiv 2507.08877](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08877v1)
- Negative findings: the first assumption, that consistent function choice marks a query as simple, had to be revised because near-identical queries sometimes got different functions from the large model (label noise from model instability); semantic clustering alone failed to generalise across entity values; the first router only covered previously seen queries, and a trained classifier added 12% offline coverage. — [arXiv 2507.08877](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08877v1)
- **Test here:** apply the ODIA filter to the ledger: keep question clusters where one recipe/method dominates and succeeded, drop mixed clusters, train logistic regression on encoder embeddings to predict "R1 to R4 will succeed with recipe X", and compare coverage and error against R2 wording match on a time-split hold-out.
- Switchcraft (1.4) is the clearest measured case of distilling LLM outcome labels into a small classifier: 77,891 training queries, DistilBERT, 80/10/10 in-distribution split plus an entirely held-out dataset. In distribution it beat kNN by 1.8 points; on the held-out set it tied kNN (89.39 to 89.88% versus 89.43%). Larger encoders gave no gain. — [arXiv 2605.07112](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07112)
- RouteNLP (1.5): targeted distillation driven by clustered escalation failures gave "over twice" the cost improvement of untargeted distillation, per the abstract; sample sizes not on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2604.23577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23577)
- Router sample-size evidence: 1K examples per dataset insufficient for a hidden-state probe, 4K adequate, 8K minimal further gain (2602.11877); 23K to 302K training queries worth +0.47 points with a frozen encoder (2606.07587). — [arXiv 2602.11877](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11877); [arXiv 2606.07587](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07587)
- Text-to-SQL distillation, generation not routing **[SNIP]**: *Knowledge Distillation with Structured Chain-of-Thought for Text-to-SQL* (Struct-SQL), arXiv 2512.17053 (Dec 2025); *SQuaD-SQL*, arXiv 2607.08161 (Jul 2026, LLM-generated synthetic data to train small text-to-SQL models). Neither opened. — [arXiv 2512.17053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17053v1); [arXiv 2607.08161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08161)
- Foundation, pre-2025 **[SNIP]**: *Towards Optimizing SQL Generation via LLM Routing*, arXiv 2411.04319 (Nov 2024), a DistilBERT classifier that picks which LLM writes the SQL on BIRD, with a "no model can" option. — [arXiv 2411.04319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04319)
- Small-plus-large collaboration for few-shot intent detection (Chen and Wang, Findings of EMNLP 2025) **[SNIP]**: small model makes the first-pass intent and out-of-scope call after LLM-assisted augmentation; LLM refines uncertain cases. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.749/)

### Inferences
- At hundreds of logged rows, the supported options are (i) kNN over the logs, (ii) a linear or tree model on frozen embeddings, (iii) using logged cases as retrieval text or few-shot examples for R3/R4. Fine-tuning a small language model for parameter filling is supported only at tens of thousands of examples in what was read.
- ODIA's label-noise finding applies directly: a ledger row saying the frontier chose method M is a noisy label. Filter to rows where the outcome was verified, or where repeated similar questions agree, before training anything.
- The local 4B model is in the same size class as UCCI's small model (4B, F1 0.847 versus 0.932 for 12B on NER). If it is asked to fill parameters, a calibrated escalation rule from its token margin is the measured pattern; an uncalibrated accept is not.
- Random splits flatter distilled routers (Switchcraft lost its edge over kNN on the held-out set). Time-split and family-split evaluation should be the default here.

### Gaps
- No paper found with a negative result specifically on distilling routing decisions from a few hundred logs; absence of evidence, not evidence that it works.
- ODIA gives no accuracy numbers, training-set totals or evaluation splits; its latency arithmetic rests on assumed per-stage times and a 60/40 traffic split.
- No paper found on distilling follow-up-question rewriting or question restatement into a small model with measured quality.
- Struct-SQL and SQuaD-SQL were not read; sample sizes and splits unknown.

## 5. Latency reduction for multi-step LLM systems without losing correctness

### Takeaway
Three techniques have measured results: speculative execution of the likely next step in parallel (about 20% time saved in the lossless setting, with extra token cost and only for side-effect-free steps), per-entry verified semantic caching (hit rates up to 57% with error under 0.5% on the authors' benchmarks), and provider prompt caching (13 to 31% faster time to first token, 41 to 80% cheaper, with the caveat that caching dynamic tool results can slow things down). No arXiv evidence was found for hedged requests in LLM ladders.

### Cited Findings

**5.1 Speculative execution**
- *Speculative Actions: A Lossless Framework for Faster Agentic Systems*, arXiv 2510.04371 (Ye et al., Columbia, 5 Oct 2025; ICLR 2026 per conference listing) **[FULL]**. A fast cheap "speculator" guesses the slow "actor's" next action; the implied calls are launched in parallel and kept only if the actor's real action matches, else discarded. Lossless means the final trajectory equals strictly sequential execution, on condition that speculated calls have no externally visible side effects or can be rolled back. — [arXiv 2510.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04371v1)
- Author results: chess (GPT-5 actor, 3 guesses, 5 runs) 54.7% guess accuracy and 19.5% time saved; τ-bench retail 22 to 38% of API calls guessed correctly; HotpotQA up to 46% with top-3 guesses under strict matching. Theoretical ceiling about 50% latency reduction under idealised assumptions (exponential latencies, independent guesses). — [arXiv 2510.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04371v1)
- Cost and limits: token spend rises with the number of guesses; in the one lossy experiment (OS tuning) actor-plus-speculator used about 608k tokens (7.83 cents) versus 63k (2.18 cents) for actor only over 200 s. Strict matching counts paraphrases as misses. Irreversible actions must not be speculated. — [arXiv 2510.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04371v1)
- **Test here:** when R3 confidence is in a middle band, start R5 SQL generation in parallel with R4; keep R5's result only if R4 fails its check. All rungs are read-only warehouse queries, so the safety condition holds. Replay the ledger to estimate saved seconds and wasted cents per question against the sequential ladder.
- *Dynamic Speculative Agent Planning*, arXiv 2509.01920 (Sep 2025) **[SNIP]**: adaptive control of how much to speculate, trading latency against cost. *PASTE*, arXiv 2603.18897 (2026) **[SNIP]**: runs predicted tool calls while the LLM is still generating. *Speculate with Memory*, arXiv 2607.12236 (Salesforce, Jul 2026) **[SNIP]**: predictions draw on past behaviour and speculation is restricted to side-effect-free operations. None opened. — [arXiv 2509.01920](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01920v1); [arXiv 2603.18897](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2603.18897); [arXiv 2607.12236](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.12236.md)
- **Test here (memory variant):** inside R6, pre-run the schema lookups and profiling queries that the ledger shows the agent nearly always issues first, in parallel with its first model call; measure wall-clock change on replayed R6 questions against the current sequential agent.

**5.2 Verified semantic caching with per-entry thresholds**
- *vCache: Verified Semantic Prompt Caching*, arXiv 2502.03771 (Schroeder et al., UC Berkeley and others, 6 Feb 2025, v5 21 Feb 2026) **[FULL, first 100k chars]**. For each cached prompt embedding, fits a sigmoid from similarity to probability the cached response is correct, by maximum likelihood on logged (similarity, correct) pairs, learned online; serves the cache only when a pessimistic estimate meets a user-set error bound δ, otherwise calls the model and uses the result as a new label. Guarantee: correct with probability at least 1 − δ, assuming i.i.d. data and that the sigmoid model fits. — [arXiv 2502.03771](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5)
- Author results: up to 12.5x higher hit rate and 26x lower error than static-threshold baselines (v5; v4 reported up to 2x and 6x, so headline figures changed between versions); 57% hit rate at under 0.5% error on SemCacheLMArena; at δ = 0.02, 0.5% error and 41% hits, where a static 0.98 threshold gave 4.1% error at 53% hits. Decision overhead under about 1.5 ms. — [arXiv 2502.03771](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5)
- Limitations: no stated minimum observations per entry (convergence about 1/√n; worked examples use 33 and 42 labels per entry); tight bounds (under about 1.5% on the classification set) force more exploration and lower hit rate; checking equivalence of long responses needs an LLM judge call; baselines were GPTCache only and one had not converged. On the classification benchmark, similarity distributions of correct and incorrect hits overlap heavily, which is the authors' argument against any single global threshold. — [arXiv 2502.03771](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5)
- **Test here:** this is R1/R2 with a learned match rule. For each saved recipe, fit the similarity-to-correct curve from ledger rows that matched it, and accept a new question only above that recipe's own pessimistic cut-off; compare false-match rate and recipe hit rate with the current wording match. Recipes with too few observations fall back to the global rule.
- "Asynchronous Verified Semantic Caching for Tiered LLM Architectures" (Feb 2026) appeared only in an aggregator listing **[SNIP, unverified]**. — [vCache search result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03771v4)

**5.3 Provider prompt caching**
- *Don't Break the Cache: An Evaluation of Prompt Caching for Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks*, arXiv 2601.06007 (Lumer et al., Jan 2026, v2 31 Jan 2026) **[SNIP of abstract-level summary]**. Three providers, four flagship models, 500+ agent sessions on DeepResearchBench with 10,000-token system prompts: cost down 41 to 80%, time to first token down 13 to 31% depending on provider. Caching everything including dynamic tool results can increase latency; caching the system prompt only was the most consistent. Benefits roughly linear in prompt size once past the provider's minimum cacheable length (ablation over 500 to 50,000 tokens and 3 to 50 tool calls). — [arXiv 2601.06007](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06007)
- **Test here:** order R5 and R6 prompts as fixed prefix (instructions, schema, method catalogue) then variable suffix (question, tool results), cache the prefix only, and read cached-token counts and time to first token from the ledger before and after.

**5.4 Small-model fast path and early exit**
- ODIA (section 4): routing 60% of function-calling traffic to a 1.3B model cut median latency 78% (1,600 ms to 350 ms) by the authors' stage-time arithmetic; accuracy not quantified. — [arXiv 2507.08877](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08877v1)
- RouteNLP (1.5): p99 1,847 ms to 387 ms in an 8-week pilot, abstract-level read. — [arXiv 2604.23577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23577)
- Switchcraft: keeping the model's KV cache across turns while routing every turn cut cost about 65% relative to uncached per-turn routing; a few expensive multi-turn queries dominated spend and pushed realised cost (27.5e-4 dollars) above the 17e-4 budget. — [arXiv 2605.07112](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07112)

### Inferences
- The 17 to 55 s at R6 is mostly sequential model turns. The published levers are, in order of evidence strength for a read-only system: fewer questions reaching R6 (sections 1, 3, 4), prefix caching inside R6, and speculative pre-execution of predictable first steps. Speculation buys time with money; the ledger can price that trade before it is built.
- The speculative-actions ceiling (about 50%, about 20% measured) means speculation alone will not turn 55 s into an interactive response; it needs to be combined with routing more questions to earlier rungs.
- Switchcraft's finding that a few queries dominate spend suggests checking whether the 25-cent, 55-second tail at R6 comes from a small identifiable cluster; if so a per-question cost cap or early stop (C3PO-style, 1.5) addresses the tail more cheaply than general speed-ups.
- vCache's per-entry threshold is the same idea as weak spot (c) applied to recipes: one cut-off per recipe learned from its own match history.

### Gaps
- No arXiv paper found on hedged or duplicated requests (send to two providers, take the first) for LLM systems with correctness accounting.
- No arXiv evidence found on early exit inside a bounded agent (stopping when a verifier is satisfied) with measured correctness loss.
- The prompt-caching paper was read only through search summaries; per-provider figures and the exact failure mode for cached tool results need the full text.
- No paper measured speculative execution in a text-to-SQL or analytics ladder; all reported numbers are from games, retail dialogue, multi-hop QA and OS tuning.
- Dynamic Speculative Agent Planning, PASTE and Speculate with Memory were not opened; their cost overhead figures are unknown.
