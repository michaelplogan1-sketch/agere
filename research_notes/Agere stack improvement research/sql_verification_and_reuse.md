# Natural-language-to-SQL correctness, verification and reuse (arXiv 2025 to November 2026)

Researched 2026-11-14. Reading depth is marked on every paper:
- **[full]** = arXiv HTML body read (methods, tables, limitations).
- **[abstract]** = arXiv abstract page only. Numbers are the authors' headline numbers; limitations were not read.
- **[snippet]** = seen only in a search result summary. Treat as a lead to verify, not as a finding.

All results are the authors' own, on the scope stated. None was measured on a healthcare warehouse unless said. "Test here" lines name the comparison against what the ladder already does.

Ladder rungs referred to below: R1 saved verified recipes, R2 hand-written recipes, R3 typed-judgment classifier, R4 small LLM parameter fill, R5 frontier LLM writes SQL blind to rows, R6 bounded frontier agent. "Promotion gate" = second model independently writes a query returning the same rows, plus regression set does not get worse.

## 1. Verifying generated SQL without a gold answer; how reliable is "two independent queries return the same rows"

### Takeaway
Same-rows-on-one-database agreement is a useful but optimistic signal: a formal verifier found that roughly one in six BIRD predictions that matched the reference on the test database were distinguishable on another database, and execution-consistency confidence reaches only 0.63 to 0.76 AUC. The strongest additions reported are (a) searching for a small database that separates the two queries, (b) checking against a different paradigm (Python over the same rows) so the two writers cannot share one misreading, and (c) a cheap learned verifier as a third signal; none removes the "both models misread the question the same way" failure.

### Cited Findings

**How optimistic is same-result agreement on a single database**
- SpotIt: Evaluating Text-to-SQL Evaluation with Formal Verification (arXiv 2510.26840, ICLR 2026) **[full, first half of HTML]**. Method: instead of comparing outputs on the static test database, a bounded equivalence verifier (extends VeriEQL) searches for a small database on which two queries give different results. Result, BIRD dev (1,533 questions), ten text-to-SQL methods: accuracy fell 9.8 to 13.5 points when the verifier replaced test-database comparison (the introduction states 11.3 to 14.2; the paper is internally inconsistent). Example: one method fell from 71.32% to 58.80%; 207 of its generated queries matched the reference on the test database but were separated by a counterexample. Spider 2.0 SQLite subset (135 questions): OmniSQL 34.1% to 22.2%, GPT-5 42.2% to 36.3%. — [SpotIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26840)
- SpotIt, cause analysis: of 36 questions that every method passed on the test database but failed under verification, manual inspection found 31 with a problematic reference SQL, 3 ambiguous questions, 2 genuine generation errors. In a 50-query sample for one method, 26% were incorrect predictions and 10% ambiguous questions; the authors say the reference SQL is the problem "more often than not". So non-equivalence is evidence that the two queries encode different readings, not proof of which one is wrong. — [SpotIt](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26840v2)
- SpotIt, limits: no window/analytic functions, no recursive CTEs, imprecise ORDER BY/LIMIT handling (can give spurious counterexamples); 52.6% of unsupported Spider 2.0 queries used window functions. Bound of 5 rows per table; most counterexamples found by bound 2; average time to a counterexample under 4 seconds; 93 to 96% of counterexamples validated by re-execution; coverage 93.6 to 97.1% of BIRD pairs. — [SpotIt](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26840v2)
- Human-Level Text-to-SQL via Reinforcement Learning on Verified Data (arXiv 2603.20004) **[abstract]**. States that result-based rewards (same rows as reference) have "non-trivial false positive rates" and adds SQL equivalence verification to the reward for that reason. Also reports that expert re-verification corrected errors in 61% of a 2.5k sample of BIRD Train. — [ReViSQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20004)
- Test: run every promotion-gate pair (R5 query, second-model query) through a bounded counterexample search or, where the SQL uses unsupported features, through generated small adversarial databases; measure how many currently promoted recipes are separated, against the current same-rows-on-warehouse gate.

**Execution self-consistency as a confidence signal**
- Confidence Estimation for Text-to-SQL in Large Language Models (arXiv 2508.14056) **[full]**. Compares logit-based, verbalised, self-check and consistency methods with no gold answer. Execution-result consistency was the best black-box method: AUC 63.25 to 71.82 on Spider dev and 66.48 to 76.34 on BIRD dev across GPT-3.5, DeepSeek, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen2.5 (the paper is inconsistent about exact model names). Calibration error stayed high (ECE roughly 17 to 34). Authors' limitations: multiple executions cost latency, "may not be suitable for large-scale enterprise databases", not evaluated on real-world databases. — [Confidence Estimation](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14056v1)
- DPC: Training-Free Text-to-SQL Candidate Selection via Dual-Paradigm Consistency (arXiv 2604.15163, ACL 2026) **[abstract]**. Names the failure directly: self-consistency and LLM-as-judge suffer "consensus on hallucinations". Method: agents build a Minimal Distinguishing Database (small, fully visible) that separates the candidates, then a solver writes an independent Python/Pandas answer on that database and candidates are checked against it. Result: up to +2.2 points absolute over self-consistency on BIRD and Spider across several LLMs. Cost and latency not stated in the abstract. — [DPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15163)
- How far do on-premises open LLMs get on Text-to-SQL? (arXiv 2606.29733) **[full]**. Five-sample self-consistency on Qwen2.5-Coder-32B, BIRD dev (n=1534): 56.13 vs 56.19 without it (-0.07) at about five times the tokens. Tested on one model only. Execution-error self-correction was the only consistently helpful step (+1.2 to +3.7 points, 1.1 to 1.5 times tokens). — [Open LLM frontier](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29733)
- CSC-SQL (arXiv 2505.13271) **[snippet]**: states self-consistency can select a suboptimal majority answer and adds a correction step. Alpha-SQL (arXiv 2502.17248) **[snippet]**: selects the candidate with highest execution self-consistency. — [CSC-SQL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13271), [Alpha-SQL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17248)
- Test: replace "second model writes SQL" with "second model writes a pandas computation over extracted base rows" for the promotion gate on a sample of new recipes; count disagreements that the SQL-vs-SQL gate passed.

**Learned verifiers and judges**
- Test-Time Verification for Text-to-SQL via Outcome Reward Models / GradeSQL (arXiv 2606.30851, ACL 2026 workshop) **[abstract]**. Trains a scorer on automatically generated candidates labelled by execution; uses it to pick among N candidates. Result: up to +4.33 points on BIRD and +2.10 on Spider over execution-based best-of-N and majority voting, open-source LLMs. Code and models stated public. — [GradeSQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30851)
- TraceSQL: Traceable Answerability Estimation for Reference-Free Text-to-SQL Verification (arXiv 2608.17795) **[abstract]**. Lightweight model over 67 explicit diagnostic features (question ambiguity, requirements, question-schema-SQL consistency, SQL structure, intent alignment). Result on BIRD dev generated SQL: 66.47% F1, 64.48% ROC-AUC vs GradeSQL-7B at 61.87% F1, 58.26% ROC-AUC. Note the absolute level: a reference-free verifier at 0.64 AUC is weak evidence on its own. — [TraceSQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17795)
- NL2SQL-BUGs (arXiv 2503.11984, KDD 2025) **[abstract]**. 2,018 expert-annotated (question, schema, SQL) instances; taxonomy of 9 main and 31 sub-categories of semantic error. LLMs averaged 75.16% detection accuracy. The benchmark also surfaced 106 annotation errors (6.91%) in BIRD. — [NL2SQL-BUGs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11984)
- Taming SQL Complexity: LLM-Based Equivalence Evaluation for Text-to-SQL (arXiv 2506.09359) **[abstract]**. Studies LLM judgment of strict and "weak" semantic equivalence and catalogues equivalent/inequivalent patterns; the abstract gives no numbers. — [Taming SQL Complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09359)
- Reflective Reasoning for SQL Generation (arXiv 2601.06678) **[snippet]**: runs without gold SQL using interpreter checks plus unsupervised "epistemic judge" feedback. — [Reflective Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06678)
- Test: take the 31-subcategory error taxonomy as a checklist for the existing typed-judgment classifier (one typed yes/no per category relevant to the question shape) and measure caught-error rate on the system's own set of known-wrong queries against the current deterministic validators alone.

**Metamorphic and counterexample testing**
- MRSQLGen, "Validating LLM-Generated SQL Queries through Metamorphic Prompting" (ACM DOI 10.1145/3797146; not arXiv) **[snippet]**: rephrases the prompt in meaning-preserving ways and compares execution results of the resulting queries. — [MRSQLGen](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3797146)
- SQLDriller (Yang et al., 2025) **[snippet, cited inside another paper]**: generates counterexample database instances to check a question against its candidate SQL. Argus (2026) **[snippet]**: LLM proposes equivalent query pairs, a SQL prover verifies them, because LLM-proposed equivalences "are not guaranteed". Both leads came via arXiv 2510.06663; primary sources not opened. — [Automated Discovery of Test Oracles](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06663)

### Inferences
- The promotion gate's exposure is specific: (1) the warehouse instance may not contain the rows that separate two different queries (empty categories, no ties, no NULLs in the relevant column, one period); (2) two models given the same schema text and definitions can share one misreading. SpotIt measures (1); DPC names (2). Neither failure is caught by the ladder's deterministic result checks, which test internal consistency of one result.
- Cheapest first step consistent with the papers: run the two queries on several small synthetic databases built to contain boundary rows (NULLs, duplicates, ties, period edges, empty groups) before promotion. This needs no prover and handles window functions, which the formal tool does not.
- Independence is stronger across paradigms than across models. A second writer producing a dataframe computation, or being given a differently worded definition, plausibly reduces shared misreading more than a second SQL writer with the identical prompt. This is an inference from DPC's design, not a measured result here.
- Majority voting over several samples of one model looks poor value (no gain at five times cost in the one full study read); the ladder's two-different-models design is not contradicted by that study, which only tested same-model samples.
- Learned verifiers reported so far sit at 0.58 to 0.76 AUC. Useful as a rank/triage input (which promoted recipes to audit first), not as an acceptance test.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures the false-acceptance rate of "two different models, same rows" directly. SpotIt measures model-vs-reference agreement; the two-model rate is an extrapolation.
- SpotIt's exact share of reference-SQL errors in the 50-query sample is in a figure not readable in the fetched text. The second half of the HTML (appendix) was not read.
- No test-case-generation paper for SQL in the CodeT sense (model writes expected outputs) was found for 2025 to 2026 beyond DPC's distinguishing database.
- Result-plausibility checks (range, trend, cross-total sanity) as a research topic: nothing found; the ladder's deterministic validators appear to be ahead of the literature located here.
- SQLDriller, Argus, MRSQLGen, CSC-SQL, Alpha-SQL and the Reflective Reasoning paper were not opened; details need verification before use.

## 2. Reusing or caching verified queries, and guards against wrong reuse

### Takeaway
The closest published designs key reuse on a canonical structured intent (measures, grouping, filters, time window) with exact match and a confidence gate, treat a false hit as worse than a miss, and report zero false hits on their own test sets; this is the same shape as R1's typed-slot recipes. Evidence that stored verified queries also help unseen questions exists for one benchmark (+4.34 points on BIRD), with verification quality mattering more than memory format.

### Cited Findings
- Semantic Caching for OLAP via LLM-Based Query Canonicalization (arXiv 2602.19811) **[abstract]**. Both SQL and natural-language requests are mapped to an "OLAP Intent Signature" (measures, grouping levels, filters, time windows). Reuse requires exact signature match under strict schema validation; natural-language requests are admitted only above a canonicalisation confidence gate; two safe derivations (roll-up, filter-down) extend coverage without approximate matching. Result on TPC-DS, SSB and NYC TLC (1,395 queries): 82% hit rate vs 28% for text keys and 56% for AST keys, zero false hits. Scope: star-schema dashboard queries. — [Semantic Caching for OLAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19811)
- Test: add roll-up and filter-down derivation to R1 (answer a coarser or narrower question from a saved recipe's signature rather than its exact slots) and measure extra R1 hit rate and wrong-answer count on the regression set against exact-shape matching.
- Conversational Query Engine for Mixed-Modality Heterogeneous Enterprise Data Sources / COGNI (arXiv 2606.28370, KDD 2026 workshop) **[abstract; cache numbers from snippet]**. Production BI system whose cache checks equivalence on several dimensions rather than embedding similarity alone, failing closed. Abstract: zero false cache hits, 8.4x latency reduction. Search snippet (not verified in the abstract): 200 evaluation queries, 7 false misses. Author affiliation not shown; treat as an industry system report. — [COGNI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28370)
- From Test-Time Scaling to Reusable Memory: Measuring Crystallization in Text-to-SQL (arXiv 2608.07213) **[abstract]**. Separates three effects of a memory of verified corrected queries: replay on recurring questions, cross-question retention, held-out same-database transfer. Result on BIRD: storing verified corrected queries raised held-out first-attempt accuracy by 4.34 points, 44.4% of the headroom that on-demand repair gives on the same questions. Controlled interventions: database-specific content is the active ingredient; reliable verification and wider retrieval coverage give supported gains; richer memory formats and elaborate retrievers do not. — [Crystallization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07213)
- Test: give R5 the nearest saved verified recipes for the same warehouse as examples and measure first-attempt correctness on held-out questions against R5 with schema and definitions only; report recurring and new questions separately, as the paper does.
- AgentSM: Semantic Memory for Agentic Text-to-SQL (arXiv 2601.15709) **[abstract]**. Stores prior or synthesised execution traces as structured programs that guide later runs. Result on Spider 2.0: 25% fewer tokens, 35% shorter trajectories, 44.8% execution accuracy on Spider 2.0 Lite. — [AgentSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15709)
- Test: seed R6 with the structured trace of the nearest previously solved question and measure tokens and steps per answer against R6 starting cold.
- ReSequel (arXiv 2606.20853) **[snippet]**: two-level cache for query rewriting, one of rewritten queries and one of validated rewritten templates; on a template hit it substitutes parameters. Targets optimisation, not question answering. — [ReSequel](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20853)
- vCache: Verified Semantic Prompt Caching (arXiv 2502.03771) **[snippet]**: learns a per-entry similarity threshold online to keep the error rate under a user-set bound; guarantee assumes i.i.d. prompts; not SQL-specific. MeanCache (arXiv 2403.02694) **[snippet]**: reports 54 false hits for an embedding-threshold cache vs 3 for theirs, with context-dependent follow-up questions as the failure case. Key-collision attack paper (arXiv 2601.23088) **[snippet]**: embedding-similarity caches can be driven to collide. — [vCache](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v3), [MeanCache](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.02694), [Key Collision](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.23088)
- Tk-Boost, "Arming Data Agents with Tribal Knowledge" (arXiv 2602.13521) **[snippet]**: mines an agent's past database interactions for recurring misconceptions and stores corrections; argues this beats retrieving raw traces. MERIT (arXiv 2606.00547) **[snippet]**: successful trajectories make cleaner reusable guidance than mixing in failed ones. — [Tk-Boost](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13521), [MERIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00547)
- KU-DMIS at EHRSQL 2024 (arXiv 2406.00014) **[abstract]**: question templatisation as the front step for EHR SQL (see section 3); the healthcare precedent for shape-keyed reuse. — [KU-DMIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00014)

### Inferences
- The literature's guard set converges on four things the ladder can check itself against: exact structured-key match (no nearest-neighbour acceptance), a confidence gate on mapping the question to the key, fail closed, and count false hits separately from misses. R1 already has the first; the measurable gap is whether R1 reports a false-hit count on a labelled set of near-miss question pairs (same shape, different period/population/measure definition).
- MeanCache's failure case applies directly: the local app now carries the last four questions of a conversation, so a follow-up ("and for last year?") can match a recipe shape while depending on context. Recipe keys should include resolved context, not the raw follow-up text.
- A per-recipe acceptance threshold learned from observed outcomes (vCache's idea) could replace one global threshold in R3's "confirm fit" step; this is a transfer by analogy and unmeasured for SQL.
- Storing "what went wrong and the correction" per warehouse (Tk-Boost, Crystallization) is a second use of the recipe store beyond replay: as grounding for R5/R6.

### Gaps
- No paper found on mining parameterised templates from SQL query logs for question answering in 2025 to 2026; searches returned agent-memory work instead.
- No published bound on wrong reuse specific to question-to-SQL caches; reported guarantees are empirical zero-false-hit counts on the authors' own sets.
- COGNI's equivalence dimensions and evaluation size, and the OLAP cache's confidence threshold (a snippet says 0.9), were not confirmed from the papers' bodies.
- Nothing found on invalidating saved queries when schema or definitions change.

## 3. EHR and healthcare question answering over structured data

### Takeaway
The transferable ideas are the reliability framing (penalise wrong answers more than abstentions), question templatisation, schema-renamed evaluation, and repeated-run consistency as a metric; 2025 to 2026 work moved from single-shot SQL to conversational and FHIR agents, where the best agents succeed in at least one of five runs far more often than in all five. Most benchmarks sit on MIMIC-IV or eICU; access terms were not verified per benchmark in this session.

### Cited Findings
- EHRSQL 2024 shared task overview (arXiv 2405.06673) **[snippet]**: a reliable model must answer correctly and also decline questions that are unanswerable or likely to be answered wrongly; built on MIMIC-IV, and the demo version was used for evaluation. — [EHRSQL 2024 overview](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06673)
- TrustSQL: Benchmarking Text-to-SQL Reliability with Penalty-Based Scoring (arXiv 2403.15879; 2024, foundation) **[abstract]**. Scores correct SQL on feasible questions and abstention on infeasible ones (schema-incompatible questions, questions needing functionality beyond SQL) with a penalty for wrong answers; compares pipelines (generator plus infeasible-question detector plus SQL-error detector) with unified models; finds high scores under severe penalties hard to reach. — [TrustSQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15879)
- Test: score the ladder's regression set with a penalty metric (correct = +1, abstain or supported unknown = 0, wrong = -c for c in {1, 10, N}) alongside plain accuracy, and compare ladder configurations on that instead of accuracy alone.
- KU-DMIS at EHRSQL 2024 (arXiv 2406.00014) **[abstract]**. Standardises questions into a templated form, then fine-tuned GPT-3.5 with table schemas in the prompt, then verification by executing the query. Reports "competitive" leaderboard results (no numbers in the abstract) and that plain fine-tuning did well on dev but struggled with out-of-domain test questions. — [KU-DMIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00014)
- CBR-to-SQL (arXiv 2603.05569) **[abstract]**. Splits retrieval in two: first structurally similar question-SQL cases, then alignment of medical entities (drug names, procedure titles) to database values. Reports accuracy "competitive" with fine-tuned methods and more robustness than single-step retrieval under data scarcity and retrieval perturbation on two clinical benchmarks (not named in the abstract; a snippet names EHRSQL as one and says the method does not address abstention). — [CBR-to-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05569)
- Test: in R3/R4, separate "which recipe shape" from "which coded value does this clinical term map to" into two steps with separate evidence, and measure slot-fill errors on drug/procedure/diagnosis terms against the current single step.
- EHR-ChatQA, "From Conversation to Query Execution" (arXiv 2509.23415, ICLR 2026) **[abstract; some details from snippet]**. Simulated-user benchmark for EHR database agents: clarify vague questions, resolve term-to-value mismatch with tools, write SQL. Two flows: incremental refinement and goal change mid-conversation. Result for o4-mini and Gemini-2.5-Flash over five trials: at least one success in five was over 90% (incremental) and 60 to 70% (adaptive), but success in all five was up to about 60 points lower. Snippet (unverified): 366 tasks on MIMIC-IV and eICU with renamed schemas so agents cannot rely on memorised schema. Code and data stated public. — [EHR-ChatQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23415)
- Test: run every R5 and R6 answer in the regression set five times and report all-five-agree rate next to single-run accuracy; an answer that changes across runs is a candidate for abstention or for the promotion gate to reject.
- FHIR-AgentBench (arXiv 2509.19319) **[snippet]**: 2,931 clinician-sourced questions grounded in MIMIC-IV-FHIR. FHIRPath-QA (arXiv 2602.23479) **[snippet]**: patient-specific questions with FHIRPath queries, from the MIMIC-IV on FHIR Demo dataset. CliniCARE-Bench (arXiv 2608.07796) **[snippet]**: 25 clinician-authored scenarios instantiated as 750 patient cases over MIMIC-IV; scores calibrated abstention, evidence grounding, repeated-run reliability and resources. — [FHIR-AgentBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19319), [FHIRPath-QA](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23479v2), [CliniCARE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07796)
- CELEC, "Reliable Curation of EHR Dataset via Large Language Models" (arXiv 2511.00772) **[snippet]**: reports 81.05% RS(0) on an adapted EHRSQL-2024 test set, after removing some unanswerable questions that failed to execute on the MIMIC-IV demo in DuckDB. — [CELEC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00772)
- Boundary-Aware NL2SQL / BAR-SQL (arXiv 2601.10318) **[abstract]** (enterprise, not healthcare). Trains a model with a reward covering both SQL execution accuracy and abstention quality on ambiguous and unanswerable questions; reports 91.48% average on the authors' own Ent-SQL-Bench and states it outperforms Claude 4.5 Sonnet and GPT-5 there. Model size not in the abstract; benchmark is the authors' own. — [BAR-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10318)

### Inferences
- What transfers to a different schema: the penalty metric, the infeasible-question categories (schema cannot answer; needs something SQL cannot do), templatisation, two-stage structure-then-entity retrieval, five-run consistency, and schema renaming as a test for memorisation. What does not transfer: any fine-tuned model or question set tied to MIMIC table names.
- EHRSQL-family questions are patient- and encounter-level hospital questions. The system's population-health, value-based-care and payment questions (cohort rates, attributed populations, period comparisons) have no located healthcare benchmark; evaluation on those must stay on the system's own regression set.
- Since the warehouse is synthetic and schemas differ, the benchmarks are more useful as sources of question types and unanswerable-question patterns than as scores to report.

### Gaps
- Credentialed access: only the EHRSQL-2024 use of the MIMIC-IV demo was confirmed (via snippet). Full MIMIC-IV and eICU are distributed through PhysioNet under credentialing and a data use agreement, and the standing rule "no new services" applies; which of EHR-ChatQA, FHIR-AgentBench and CliniCARE-Bench need the full credentialed data was not verified here. Check each repository's data instructions before any use.
- No 2025 to 2026 claims-data, pharmacy-fill or payer analytics text-to-SQL benchmark was found.
- Per-model numbers for EHR-ChatQA and exact EHRSQL-2024 leaderboard scores were not read.
- No healthcare paper found that evaluates reuse of saved verified queries.

## 4. Ambiguous questions; schema linking and semantic-layer grounding for definitions

### Takeaway
Models asked to produce SQL directly almost never cover all readings of an ambiguous question (0.4 to 0.9% full coverage on one benchmark); generating the readings in plain language first and parsing each raises at-least-one-reading coverage to 84% but full coverage stays under 20%. For definitions, one paired study found a 4 KB written definitions document added 17 to 23 points and outweighed the choice among three frontier models, while schema pruning gave no accuracy gain in the one full study read.

### Cited Findings
- Disambiguate First, Parse Later (arXiv 2502.18448v2) **[full]**. Method: a small LLM (Llama-3.1 8B) lists the plain-language interpretations it favours; a LoRA-tuned infilling model adds missing ones (trained with labels from comparing SQL execution results); a parser (Qwen2.5-Coder 32B) converts each to SQL. Coverage results (single = at least one correct reading / full = all readings): Ambrosia ambiguous subset (about 1K questions) zero-shot end-to-end 29.4 / 0.9; end-to-end fine-tuned 38.0 / 0.4; interpretation prompt without infilling 81.9 / 16.9; proposed 84.4 / 18.8. AmbiQT (about 1.8K): proposed 92.3 / 53.2 vs end-to-end fine-tuned 82.1 / 63.2 (fine-tuned wins on full coverage there). With gold interpretations, Ambrosia full coverage is only 49.0%, so parsing is also a limit. Limitations stated: infilling needs reference interpretations and SQL for training; wrong interpretations are not filtered; sometimes produces several interpretations for unambiguous questions; extra compute. — [Disambiguate First](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18448v2)
- Test: before R5 writes SQL, have it list plain-language readings of the question; when more than one survives, write a query per reading and show them as alternatives. Measure against current R5 on a labelled set of ambiguous warehouse questions (single and full coverage) and on unambiguous ones (false-alternative rate).
- AMBROSIA (arXiv 2406.19073; 2024, foundation) **[snippet]**: three ambiguity types (scope, attachment, vagueness) with plain-language interpretations; AmbiQT covers column/table synonym, join and precomputed-aggregate ambiguity. — [AMBROSIA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19073)
- AmbiSQL (arXiv 2508.15276, SIGMOD 2026 demo) **[abstract]**. Detects ambiguity from database elements and from LLM reasoning using a fine-grained taxonomy, asks the user multiple-choice questions, rewrites the question. Demo paper, 40 ambiguous queries from two benchmarks, no quantitative results in the abstract; integrated with the authors' commercial backend. — [AmbiSQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15276)
- "Two Surfaces of Ambiguity: Complementary Detection for Text-to-SQL" (Liu and Erdelt, VLDB 2026 AIDB workshop; not arXiv) **[title only; PDF did not decode]**. Snippet says it evaluates on AMBROSIA's 1,149 ambiguous questions with AmbiSQL as baseline. — [Two Surfaces](https://vldb.org/2026/Workshops/VLDB-Workshops-2026/AIDB/aidb26_10.pdf)
- "Probing Ambiguity: Adversarial Intent Resolution for Robust Text-to-SQL" (arXiv 2610.24248) **[snippet]**: evaluates on AMBROSIA and AmbiQT; not opened. — [Probing Ambiguity](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24248)
- SpotIt (above) supplies a detection signal: among queries that agreed on the test database but were separable, a share traced to ambiguous questions (3 of 36 in the all-methods set; 10% in a 50-query sample). — [SpotIt](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26840v2)
- Semantic Layers for Reliable LLM-Powered Data Analytics (arXiv 2604.25149) **[abstract]**. Paired single-shot design: Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4 on 100 questions over the Cleaned Contoso Retail dataset in ClickHouse, with schema only vs schema plus a 4 KB hand-written markdown file of measures, conventions and disambiguation rules. Accuracy 45.5 to 50.5% without, 67.7 to 68.7% with (+17 to +23 points, p < 0.01); models indistinguishable within each condition. One dataset, 100 questions, retail; author affiliations not shown, possible commercial interest not checked. — [Semantic Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149)
- Test: ablate the definitions text given to R5 (none, current, current plus explicit disambiguation rules per measure) on the regression set and compare with swapping the R5 model; this tells whether money is better spent on definitions or on model tier.
- Bootstrapping Semantic Layer from Execution / GATE (arXiv 2606.05634) **[abstract]**. When a definition is under-specified, keep several grounding hypotheses for the SQL component, execute the already-grounded parts, keep the hypothesis the observation supports, and store the outcome as a reusable memory entry (what was tested, how to write it). Abstract says it "consistently improves over strong baselines" on real-world and controlled benchmarks; no numbers. Requires reading query results, so it fits R6, not row-blind R5. — [GATE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05634)
- Test: let R6 record each resolved grounding (term, hypotheses tried, observation, chosen SQL fragment) as a candidate definition entry, promoted under the same two-model gate; measure R6 steps and R5 success on later questions that use the same term.
- Schema linking: in the open-LLM BIRD study **[full]**, lexical schema pruning gave no gain and one significant loss (-4.24 points for Llama-3.3-70B), saving about 16% of tokens; embedding linking raised gold-table recall from 94.0% to 96.5% with no accuracy change (-0.39, p = 0.56). — [Open LLM frontier](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29733)
- Contrary lead: "Rethinking Schema Linking: A Context-Aware Bidirectional Retrieval Approach" (arXiv 2510.14296) **[snippet]** reports its retrieved schema beats the full schema across tested LLMs. AutoLink (arXiv 2511.17190), EviLink (arXiv 2605.29670), AttnLink (arXiv 2608.00693) **[snippet]** target very large schemas. — [Bidirectional Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14296), [AutoLink](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17190), [AttnLink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00693)

### Inferences
- The promotion gate doubles as an ambiguity detector: when two independently written queries disagree, the first hypothesis to test is "two readings", and the cheapest resolution is to have each writer state its reading in one plain sentence and compare the sentences. This follows from SpotIt's cause analysis combined with the interpretation-first method; no paper tests this combination.
- Full coverage of readings is unsolved (under 20% on Ambrosia), so "present alternatives" should be framed to the user as "readings we found", not "all readings".
- Evidence on schema pruning conflicts across papers and is BIRD-specific; for a single known warehouse with a curated definitions layer, the semantic-document result suggests definitions matter more than pruning. Both are untested here.

### Gaps
- No quantitative ambiguity-detection precision/recall could be read (AmbiSQL abstract has none; the VLDB workshop PDF did not decode; arXiv 2610.24248 not opened).
- No paper found on ambiguity specific to healthcare measure definitions (attribution, continuous enrolment, index dates, period conventions).
- GATE's benchmarks and effect sizes were not read.
- The semantic-layer study's hallucination results and author affiliations were not read.

## 5. Small or fine-tuned small models versus frontier models on constrained SQL tasks

### Takeaway
No located paper measures small models on parameter filling or template selection specifically; the evidence is on full SQL generation, where fine-tuned or reinforcement-trained small models report 67 to 68% on BIRD dev (3B) against 43 to 55% for untuned open models of 7B to 70B in zero-shot. The gap between those two figures, not a frontier comparison, is the usable finding.

### Cited Findings
- FINER-SQL: Boosting Small Language Models for Text-to-SQL (arXiv 2605.03465) **[abstract]**. Reinforcement learning (GRPO, no critic) on a 3B model with dense rewards: a memory reward aligning reasoning with verified traces and an atomic reward giving partial credit for operation-level overlap. Reports "up to" 67.73% execution accuracy on BIRD and 85% on Spider at 5.57 s per sample, and says this matches much larger models (comparison models not named in the abstract). — [FINER-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03465)
- How far do on-premises open LLMs get on Text-to-SQL? (arXiv 2606.29733) **[full]**. Zero-shot, one prompt, BIRD dev n=1534: Qwen2.5-Coder 7B 42.63, 14B 52.02, 32B 54.89; CodeLlama 7B 23.73, 13B 26.27; Llama-3.1-8B 37.09; Llama-3.3-70B 54.69. With execution-error self-correction: 46.54, 53.52, 56.19, 27.12, 30.64, 41.07, 55.67. No frontier models, few-shot or fine-tuning tested. Run-to-run variation flips 0.2 to 3.7% of items, so differences under about one point are not interpretable. A provider defect (dropped spaces next to digits) cost about three points until repaired. — [Open LLM frontier](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29733)
- ReViSQL (arXiv 2603.20004) **[abstract]**. Training-data quality is the stated bottleneck: fine-tuning on 2.5k expert-verified examples beat training on the noisy full set by 11 to 16 points on Arcwise-Plat and Spider2; reports 92.96% on Arcwise-Plat. Uses very large open models (Qwen3-235B, Kimi-K2.6), so this is evidence about verified data, not small models. — [ReViSQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20004)
- BAR-SQL (arXiv 2601.10318) **[abstract]**: a fine-tuned model reported to beat Claude 4.5 Sonnet and GPT-5 on the authors' own enterprise benchmark including abstention; model size not stated in the abstract. — [BAR-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10318)
- KU-DMIS (arXiv 2406.00014) **[abstract]**: fine-tuned GPT-3.5 did well on EHRSQL dev but plain fine-tuning struggled on out-of-domain test questions; templatisation was their remedy. — [KU-DMIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00014)
- Semantic Layers study (arXiv 2604.25149) **[abstract]**: within one tier, three frontier models were statistically indistinguishable with or without the definitions document. A snippet attributes to the paper the claim that a smaller model with semantic context beats a larger one without; in the abstract the supported comparison is Sonnet 4.6 with the document (about 68%) vs Opus 4.7 without (about 46 to 50%). — [Semantic Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149)
- TraceSQL (arXiv 2608.17795) **[abstract]**: a lightweight feature model outscored a 7B learned verifier on reference-free verification (64.48 vs 58.26 ROC-AUC, BIRD dev), a case of a small explicit model beating a larger one on a constrained judgment. — [TraceSQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17795)
- CHS-SQL (arXiv 2607.22624) **[snippet]**: fine-tuned small models with confidence-guided schema linking, "close to" large models on a single RTX 4090; not opened. Arctic-Text2SQL-R1 is cited in the open-LLM paper at 70.5% for 32B under a different protocol; not opened. — [CHS-SQL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22624), [Open LLM frontier](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29733)

### Inferences
- Parameter filling and recipe selection are narrower than full SQL generation, so small-model results on BIRD are a lower bound on what R3/R4 could do; this is reasoning, not evidence. The direct test is cheap: replay logged R4 slot fills and R3 selections through a smaller or fine-tuned model and compare exact-slot agreement and downstream answer correctness with the current model.
- The recipe store plus promotion gate produces exactly the kind of small, verified, warehouse-specific training set ReViSQL and FINER-SQL say matters (verified examples and verified traces). Whether a few hundred to a few thousand verified recipes suffice for tuning a small R4 is untested.
- Out-of-domain fragility of fine-tuned models (KU-DMIS) argues for keeping the ladder's escalation: a tuned small model on in-shape questions, with fit confirmation and fall-through to R5.
- Benchmark noise matters when comparing ladder configurations: with 0.2 to 3.7% run-to-run flips reported, regression comparisons need repeated runs or paired tests.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for small models on SQL slot filling or template selection as such.
- No same-protocol head-to-head of a fine-tuned small model against a current frontier model was read in full; FINER-SQL's and BAR-SQL's comparisons are abstract-level and on the authors' chosen settings.
- Papers with Code leaderboards for BIRD and Spider 2.0 were not consulted in this session; no leaderboard figures are reported here.
- FINER-SQL training cost, and whether its 67.73% is a single best run, were not read.
