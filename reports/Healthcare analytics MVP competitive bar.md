# Win pitches by publishing proof competitors withhold

A tiered, verified analytics stack wins pitches against population-health and data platforms by delivering three things together. The first is correct, reconcilable answers to the table-stakes value-based care questions: attribution, total cost of care per member per month, HCC risk under V28, quality measures, utilisation and readmissions, and shared-savings projection. The second is an accuracy and cost evidence pack measured the way the research literature says claims should be measured, which no competitor publishes today. The third is a 60 to 90 day paid pilot tied to one outcome metric the buyer already tracks. The opening is real and specific. Databricks and Snowflake have built the strongest trust machinery (governed metrics, curated question-and-SQL pairs, regression evaluation), but they ship it empty, and buyers must spend unbudgeted curation effort to move from roughly 50-70% accuracy on a fresh deployment to about 90%. Innovaccer, Arcadia, Lightbeam, Health Catalyst and Epic ship healthcare data models and agents, but none publicly documents a verified-answer mechanism or any accuracy figure for its conversational Q&A. Agere's design (saved verified recipes, typed-judgment routing, frontier SQL as a last resort, supported unknowns) is the combination neither group offers: healthcare semantics already curated, plus measured, abstention-aware accuracy. The MVP bar is therefore not more features. It is a frozen, held-out VBC question set with repeated runs, scoring that penalises wrong answers below abstentions, per-answer lineage and cost, and a demo and pilot built to clear security review, the AI governance committee and the CFO's ROI threshold of about 3x. Every competitor figure below is vendor-reported unless stated, and nothing here has yet been measured on Agere.

## Horizontal platforms set a 90% bar that only curation reaches

The general data platforms are furthest along on governed natural-language Q&A, and their architecture looks a lot like Agere's top rungs. Snowflake Cortex Analyst generates SQL over a semantic view and can return a **verified query**, a curated question/SQL pair carrying `verified_at` and `verified_by` fields, directly when a user asks a similar question ([QueryPlane](https://queryplane.com/blog/snowflake-cortex-analyst-in-practice/)). Its built-in evaluation runs generated SQL against those verified queries as ground truth and reports accuracy and regressions, but the evaluation sets are curated by hand ([Snowflake docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-analyst-evaluations)). Databricks Genie grounds answers in Unity Catalog metric views and trusted assets. It labels a response **"Trusted" only when it uses the exact text of a curated example query or SQL function**, which is a provenance signal rather than a measured accuracy ([Databricks Genie docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/genie/)). Genie benchmarks compare results with optional gold SQL that is withheld from Genie's context, and Databricks recommends two to four phrasings per question ([Databricks benchmarks docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/genie/benchmarks)).

Their headline numbers are all vendor-run. On November 11, 2026, Databricks reported Genie with its new Ontology passing **91.0% of tasks against 45.0% and 58.0% for two unnamed frontier agents**, with less than half the tokens. The test ran on a fictional energy company, five runs per task, with an undisclosed task count, and the authors caution that gaps may shrink in new deployments ([Databricks blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/genie-genie-ontology-delivers-16-accuracy-less-half-tokens)). Genie One's 84.5% came from a 28-question internal suite ([Databricks Genie One](https://www.databricks.com/blog/introducing-genie-one-genie-ontology-and-genie-agents)). Snowflake cited about 90% on roughly 150 internal questions against 51% for single-shot GPT-4o ([Snowflake engineering blog](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/engineering/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi/)). Field evidence shows what sits underneath these numbers. One consultancy got **8 of 15 right on a fresh Genie space and 15 of 15 only after remodelling metadata** ([Syrencloud](https://syrencloud.com/?p=7319)). Practitioners call human curation the "largest unbudgeted cost" of both products, and Genie is documented as nondeterministic, limited to 30 tables per space (Databricks advises five or fewer) and 20 UI questions per minute per workspace ([Colrows](https://colrows.com/blogs/cortex-analyst-vs-genie/)). Pricing is per message on Snowflake: about 6.7 AI credits per 100 messages at $2.00 per credit, roughly **$0.13 per question plus warehouse compute**. Genie has no per-question charge but bills SQL warehouse runtime ([Colrows](https://colrows.com/blogs/cortex-analyst-vs-genie/)).

The healthcare vendors compete on prebuilt data models, measures and agents, and they say almost nothing about whether an answer is correct. Innovaccer's Population Health Copilot 2.0 promises "ask your data anything" with chain-of-thought steps shown, and makes no accuracy claim and describes no verification ([Innovaccer blog](https://innovaccer.ai/blogs/population-health-copilot-2-0-is-now-faster-smarter-and-more-intuitive)). Its 2026 Gravity platform adds more than 50 prebuilt agents, PHI redaction and audit logs ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260729355311/en/Innovaccer-Named-a-Leader-in-the-Inaugural-Gartner-Magic-Quadrant-for-Healthcare-Provider-Industry-Cloud-Platforms)). Arcadia's October 2026 Ascent suite pairs "conversational AI" with actuarial modelling, multi-payer reconciliation and an autonomous care-navigation agent. It has the most explicit activation policy in the market: **agents start assistive and act autonomously only after performance thresholds are proven, with a tamper-evident audit trail** ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/10/05/arcadia-launches-ascent-carejourney-lifescience-agentic-population-health-platform/)). Lightbeam showed Analytics Copilot and Cohort Copilot on Microsoft's marketplace at HIMSS26 and published no detail on their data scope or accuracy ([Lightbeam press release](https://lightbeamhealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Press-Release-LB-HIMSS-2026-Highlights.pdf)). No shipped natural-language assistant was found for Health Catalyst Ignite, only a 2025 roadmap item ([Health Catalyst roadmap](https://docs.healthcatalyst.com/getting-started/articles/media/product-roadmaps.pdf)). Epic previewed Cogito Assistant and Cosmos Curiosity for population-level questions at UGM 2026 ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidchou/2026/08/25/epics-big-bet-three-themes-from-ugm-2026/)).

| Vendor | AI analytics today | Verified or reusable answers | Published Q&A accuracy | Main documented limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snowflake Cortex Analyst | NL to SQL over semantic views | Verified queries; regression evals | ~90% internal, vendor-run | Buyer curates semantics; Snowflake-only |
| Databricks Genie | NL to SQL, Ontology, agents | Metric views, "Trusted" label, benchmarks | 84.5-91% internal, vendor-run | Nondeterministic; ≤30 tables; curation cost |
| Innovaccer | Copilot, 50+ agents, Atlas for VBC | Steps shown, audit logs | None | No verification described |
| Arcadia | Ascent Insight, care and finance agents | Audit trail; assistive-first activation | None | No verified-answer mechanism described |
| Lightbeam | Analytics and Cohort Copilots | Not documented | None | No public detail |
| Health Catalyst | Ignite plus Healthcare.AI | Not documented | None | NL assistant unconfirmed |
| Epic | Cogito Assistant, Cosmos Curiosity | Not documented | None | Readmission handled by risk models |

Two gaps stand out. **No healthcare vendor publishes Q&A accuracy, and no independent benchmark of Genie or Cortex Analyst on claims, EHR or quality-measure data exists.** Readmission and VBC analytics from these vendors means prebuilt risk models, worklists and contract tooling, and ad hoc, sourced answers to new readmission or VBC questions are not evidenced anywhere. Their outcome claims share the same weakness. Lightbeam's "41% average relative reduction in avoidable admissions" is an unweighted mean of two clients (130 and 65 admissions avoided), with no denominators, definition, comparison group or validator ([Lightbeam](https://lightbeamhealth.com/news/lightbeam-ai-model-enables-average-41-relative-reduction-in-avoidable-admissions/)).

## Buyers pay for VBC numbers they can reconcile, at roughly 3x ROI

Demand comes from risk-bearing contracts, and those contracts are growing and getting riskier. The Medicare Shared Savings Program reached **511 ACOs and 12.6 million assigned beneficiaries on January 1, 2026**, both records ([MedPAC Data Book](https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/July2026_MedPAC_DataBook_Sec5_SEC.pdf)). The CY2026 rule cut new ACOs' one-sided time from seven years to five ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-11-05/html/2025-19787.htm)). ACO REACH ends December 31, 2026 and is replaced by LEAD, which runs from 2027 to 2036 with **benchmarks locked for the decade and no mid-model rebasing** ([Health Affairs Forefront](https://www.healthaffairs.org/do/10.1377/forefront.20260302.627865/full/)). V28 risk adjustment is fully phased in for 2026, and CMS will exclude audio-only and unlinked chart-review diagnoses from 2027 risk scores ([CMS memo](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2026-risk-adjustment-implementation-memo-g.pdf); [HCC Buddy](https://hccbuddy.com/news/cms-2027-rate-announcement-audio-only-unlinked-crr-exclusions)). The APP Plus quality set is in a collection-type transition, with MIPS CQMs scheduled to sunset after PY2026 and a 2027 proposal to extend them ([CMS](https://cms.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/py2026_performance_score_40th_percentile.pdf); [Applied Policy](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/wp-content/uploads/CY-2027-PFS_MSSP-Detailed-Summary-7.20.26.pdf)). Readmissions are near-universal but shallow: **2,334 hospitals (80%) are penalised in FY2027, most by under 1%**, with 10 at the 3% cap ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/hospital-readmission-reduction-program-penalties-rise-2027-cms/832473/)). Readmission analytics is therefore a required module rather than a purchase driver. Its larger dollar value sits inside total cost of care under ACO and LEAD contracts, where an average readmission costs about **$16,300** ([AHRQ HCUP](https://hcup-us.ahrq.gov/reports/statbriefs/sb307-readmissions-2020.jsp)).

From this, the table stakes every serious evaluation will test follow directly. They are: attribution and roster reconciliation, since a roster built mainly from CCLF files is "likely wrong" because CCLF includes beneficiaries who visited but are not assigned ([Tuva Project](https://thetuvaproject.com/understanding-medicare-aco-assignment)); TCOC and PMPM against benchmark, with lag-aware completion because claims arrive two to three months late ([Cedar Gate](https://cedargate.com/resources/the-ideal-tech-setup-for-aco-reach-success-data-analytics-care-payment)); RAF under V28; APP Plus eCQMs or HEDIS/Stars; care gaps; ED, inpatient and readmission utilisation; and shared-savings projection. Buyers do not lack dashboards. CHIME's 2025 Most Wired survey found nearly all organisations have them and that outcomes depend on operationalising them, with data-quality confidence linked to AI adoption ([CHIME/DHI](https://dhinsights.org/insight/2025-most-wired-analytics-and-data-management-trends-report/)). Their pains are trust and plumbing. Only **20% of healthcare executives fully trust their EHR data** for strategic decisions ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/most-health-execs-don-t-trust-hospital-data-inform-their-strategic-decisions)), and about 57% of health-system data is used for decisions ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/report-only-57-of-healthcare-organizations-data-is-used-to-make-decisions-301886374.html)). KLAS feedback on incumbents is about cost and roadmap, not AI quality: nearly a third of Health Catalyst customers plan to leave over data-storage costs, and Arcadia customers cite staff turnover ([HIT Consultant on KLAS](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/05/02/klas-report-which-health-analytics-vendors-deliver-results/)).

Governance is now a formal gate. **72% of organisations run AI purchases through governance committees** ([Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-2026-healthcare-ai-roi-scorecard)). The Joint Commission and CHAI guidance of September 2025 asks health systems to obtain vendor validation and bias evidence, validate locally on a representative sample, and monitor drift with intensity scaled to risk ([Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/new-guidance-on-responsible-ai-use-in-healthcare-what-health-tech-companies-should-know/)). The CHAI Applied Model Card (intended use, population, data, performance metrics, known risks, out-of-scope uses) is the common disclosure template ([HC Innovation Group](https://hcinnovationgroup.com/analytics-ai/data-information-governance/article/55236650/coalition-proposes-nutrition-label-for-health-ai)). ONC's HTI-5 final rule removed federal model-card requirements for certified EHRs, which moves assurance onto these voluntary frameworks and internal committees ([Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/onc-issues-hti-5-final-rule-removing-model-card-and-risk-management-requirements-for-predictive-decision-support-interventions)). Separately from the engineering work, buyers will ask for a BAA, SOC 2 Type II or HITRUST, a subprocessor list, PHI processing locations, and a statement that customer PHI is not used for training ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/how-healthcare-organizations-must-vet-ai-vendors-that-overstate-their-compliance); [StartupMD](https://blog.thestartupmd.com/blog/what-is-a-health-it-vendor-assessment)). These are deployment prerequisites, not product acceptance. A stack that can run inside the buyer's own warehouse shortens that review, though no source quantified the preference.

The money test is blunt. Buyers want **about 3x realised return before funding at scale and realise it in roughly 12 months**, and only 23% of systems reporting AI ROI can quantify it ([Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-2026-healthcare-ai-roi-scorecard); [Perioperative Leader](https://www.periopleader.com/briefs/ai-vendors-bet-on-guaranteed-roi-as-competition-with-epic-intensifies)). Trust outranks price: Menlo found buyers pay a premium for AI they trust and treat cost as secondary ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-ai-in-healthcare/)). Bessemer's clinical-trust drivers translate directly into product requirements: validation evidence (67%), transparent rationale (53%), patient-specific performance (47%) and EHR integration (44%) ([Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-2026-healthcare-ai-roi-scorecard)).

## Public benchmarks overstate warehouse accuracy by 50 points or more

Any accuracy pitch has to survive a sophisticated buyer who knows the benchmark gap. Leaderboard entries reach about 94-97% on Spider 2.0-Snow and 70-76% on Spider 2.0-Lite, all self-submitted and undated ([Spider 2.0 leaderboard](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)). On BEAVER, built from two real enterprise warehouses, **agentic frameworks on GPT-5.2 reach 10.8% execution accuracy, and 30.1% even with oracle hints** ([BEAVER](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02038v2)). On interactive tasks, GPT-5 succeeds 8.67% of the time in conversational mode ([BIRD-Interact](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05318v3)). Population-health questions, with proprietary schemas, ambiguous definitions and multi-step attribution logic, look like BEAVER, not Spider. Healthcare results split the same way. Claude Sonnet 4 answered 94 of 100 answerable EHRSQL questions ([M3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01053)). On EHR-ChatQA's simulated vague or shifting questions, however, **success in all five of five trials ran 35-60 points below success in at least one** ([EHR-ChatQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23415)). No claims, attribution or VBC text-to-SQL benchmark exists, so a buyer-specific question set is the only valid scoreboard.

The literature also says what makes a claim credible, and the market bar sits well below it. Gold labels are unreliable: a UIUC audit found **52.8% of BIRD Mini-Dev examples contain annotation errors**, and correcting them moved agent rankings by up to nine places ([Jin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778)). The EHRSQL 2024 shared task scored abstention with TrustSQL's Reliability Score. Under it, a correct answer or a correct abstention earns credit, any wrong answer is penalised, and under RS(N) a single error can outweigh every correct answer ([EHRSQL 2024 overview](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06673)). Whole-task scoring changes conclusions: on HealthAdminBench, GPT-5.4 passed 82.8% of subtasks but completed 26.7% of tasks ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/measuring-what-medical-ai-can-actually-do)). An evidence pack that exceeds the market bar therefore contains six things. The first is a frozen, held-out question set written from the buyer's own intents and never used to tune recipes. The second is result-set comparison against SQL audited by a second person. The third is several phrasings per intent. The fourth is at least three runs, reported as all-runs consistency. The fifth is unanswerable and ambiguous questions scored with an abstention-aware metric such as RS(10). The sixth is a per-question error taxonomy. Because the prevailing vendor standard is a few dozen to 150 unreleased questions, an anonymised competitor and one headline number ([Pebblous](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/databricks-genie-one-governed-data/en/)), this pack is itself a differentiator.

The same evidence supports Agere's tiering as the cost story. Curated semantics raise accuracy in every published ablation. Snowflake's BIRD test moved Claude 3.5 Sonnet from 57% to 78% with a semantic model, and dbt's 2026 run moved Claude Sonnet 4.6 from 90.0% raw to 98.2% with a modeled layer, though both used small question sets ([Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/snowflake/cortex-analyst-vs-text-to-sql/)). Reuse is safe when matching is strict. A structured-intent cache reached an **82% hit rate with zero false hits**, but turning plain language into the key was right only 44.4% of the time, so ambiguous wording must bypass the cache ([OLAP semantic caching](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19811v1)). Frontier agent tasks cost about $0.06-0.18 each on LiveSQLBench ([benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/livesqlbench/)), and LLM-written queries showed up to 3.4x variance in warehouse cost, so a cost claim must count bytes scanned as well as tokens ([Cost-Aware Text-to-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22364v1)). Chart reading is where cost explodes: an inpatient stay averages about **75K-108K input tokens and reaches 1.4M**, while retrieving about 7.7K tokens kept F1 within a few points ([RAG vs long context](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14817v1)). The defensible cost claim is mechanical, not a headline. Report the share of held-out traffic answered by a saved recipe at no model cost, the measured false-reuse rate, and the token and compute cost per answer at each rung. No peer-reviewed end-to-end comparison of recipe reuse against calling a frontier model on every question exists, so Agere's own measurement is the claim.

Earlier research on this stack points to the same weakness in a different place. "The query ran" and "two models returned the same rows" are softer labels than they look. A formal verifier that searches for databases on which two queries differ cut ten methods' measured accuracy by 9.8-13.5 points ([SpotIt](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26840v2)). Labels calibrated on execution carried 17-20 points of risk once two SQL experts relabelled the same items blind ([Certified Against Which Oracle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25938)). A hand-checked sample of a few hundred answers is therefore what turns Agere's verification story from plausible into provable in front of a buyer.

## The winning demo shows the receipt behind every number

Buyers are flooded: 57% say they get too many point-solution pitches, and they are moving from evaluating capabilities to buying implementations ([Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-2026-healthcare-ai-roi-scorecard)). A winning demo therefore runs on a workflow, not a feature tour. It opens with a question the VBC or finance lead already asks, for example "which attributed members drove the rise in PMPM this quarter, and how much of it was avoidable readmissions?". It answers in seconds from a verified recipe and shows the receipt: population and date scope, measure definition and version, lineage to source rows, recipe identity and verification record, rung used, and cost of the answer. It drills from cohort to patient, which addresses the "patient-specific performance" trust driver. It then asks a new question that no recipe covers, so the buyer watches the ladder escalate, the frontier SQL get checked, and either a sourced answer or a supported "unknown" with the reason. It closes by re-running the held-out scorecard live and showing where the system abstained and why. Every competitor can show a fluent answer. Only this demo shows a wrong answer being refused, which speaks directly to post-Olive scepticism about AI promises ([Haverin](https://haverin.substack.com/p/after-olive-why-healthcare-leaders)). The demo should stay framed as non-patient-facing decision support for population and finance teams, because Menlo finds lower patient risk means faster approval ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-ai-in-healthcare/)). The research found no buyer survey asking for "AI against analyst" or "cost per answer" displays. Those elements follow from the 23% ROI-quantification gap, but they are an inference.

Synthetic data is a legitimate demo substrate as long as the demo says the figures are illustrative. EHR sandboxes themselves run on Synthea, whose maintainers say its models are better for showing how concepts are represented than for real insights ([Synthea FAQ](https://github.com/synthetichealth/synthea/wiki/Frequently-Asked-Questions)). What the demo sells is mechanics, provenance and refusal behaviour. The natural next step is a pilot that re-runs the same question set on the buyer's own extract.

The pilot is where deals are won or lost. Piloting is common and production is rare: **28% of providers and 24% of payers are piloting AI, but only 12% and 6% are in full production** ([Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-2026-healthcare-ai-roi-scorecard)). Buying cycles run about 6.6 months for AI at health systems and 11.3 months at payers ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-ai-in-healthcare/)). The structure that converts has several parts. It is paid, even nominally, with the fee credited to year one. It lasts 60-90 days with a hard end date. It has one outcome metric with a measured baseline, two adoption metrics and a quality metric, a midpoint go/no-go call, a named economic buyer, and **a conversion clause fixing price, term and start date before kickoff** ([LeadsAce](https://leadsace.io/why-pilots-dont-convert); [Clunic](https://clunic.com/blog/ai-agent-pilot-metrics)). The pilot should also produce evidence for each hospital buying gate: champion, budget holder, IT and security, AI governance, contracting and adoption ([The Growth Syndicate](https://www.thegrowthsyndicate.com/resources/selling-to-hospitals)). Expect a head-to-head comparison. Cleveland Clinic tested five ambient scribes on more than 300 clinicians before choosing one ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/cleveland-clinic-chooses-ambience-ai-scribe)), so Agere should plan to be scored against the buyer's analysts or an incumbent copilot on the same held-out questions.

For Agere, the pilot's outcome metric should be one the VBC lead already funds. Strong candidates are analyst turnaround on ad hoc TCOC and readmission questions, reconciliation of attributed lives and PMPM to CMS reports, or care-gap and readmission worklists acted on. The quality metric should be the held-out scorecard (correct, abstained, wrong) re-run weekly. On pricing, the market is annual subscriptions scaled to covered lives, with third-party estimates of about $0.50-$10 PMPM and Arcadia contracts of $250K-$1.5M a year. These figures are unverified ([ITQlick](https://www.itqlick.com/arcadia-io/pricing); [MedicalRecords.com](https://www.medicalrecords.com/ai-tools/pricing/population-health)). Risk-sharing is spreading, from 120-day ROI guarantees to pay-after-savings ([Perioperative Leader](https://www.periopleader.com/briefs/ai-vendors-bet-on-guaranteed-roi-as-competition-with-epic-intensifies)). A platform fee plus a low PMPM, with a variable portion tied to an agreed savings attribution method, fits that pattern without the cash-flow risk of outcome-only fees. Shared-savings economics keep the price honest: MSSP net savings were **$241 per beneficiary in PY2024** ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/cms-acos-generated-2-4b-in-medicare-savings-in-2024)), so for a 10,000-life ACO the fee must be a small fraction of the incremental savings it can credibly claim.

## The MVP bar maps onto the ladder Agere already has

Mapped against all of this, the MVP has a short list of things it must do and a shorter list of things it must not claim.

| Requirement | Why buyers need it | Agere component that delivers it | Proof to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correct table-stakes VBC answers (attribution, TCOC/PMPM, RAF V28, APP Plus/HEDIS, utilisation, readmission, shared savings) | Every RFP tests these | Verified SQL recipes over governed definitions | Reconciliation to CMS-style reports; recipe verification record |
| New questions answered or refused, never guessed | No competitor documents this | Typed-judgment router, small model, frontier SQL with checks, supported unknown | Held-out set with RS(10)-style scoring, unanswerable items included |
| Consistency, not one lucky run | pass^k gaps of 35-60 points | Deterministic recipe replay; repeated runs on higher rungs | All-runs consistency per rung |
| Lineage and rationale per figure | Bessemer trust drivers; data-trust gap | Shared decision context: question, scope, evidence version, interpretation | Receipt on every answer |
| Cost per answer | Cost claims must include compute | Tiering; recipe hits avoid model calls | Share of traffic at each rung, false-reuse rate, tokens plus bytes scanned |
| Chart and document evidence that supports the claim | Whole-chart prompts are 75K-1.4M tokens | Code-first chart readers with quotes | Trap set for negation and history; per-patient token cost |
| Governance artefacts | Committees, Joint Commission/CHAI | Audit log, staged activation of candidates | Model card per AI function; drift monitoring on the scorecard |
| Operational action, not another dashboard | CHIME: dashboards exist, action lags | Work and follow-up bound to the decision context | Worklist acted on during pilot |

Three things should stay out of the pitch until they are measured. The first is any accuracy number from a public benchmark or from Agere's own tuned regression set, because the first is an upper bound and the second is optimistic by construction. The second is clinical-outcome claims, since even incumbents' admission-reduction figures lack comparison groups. The third is autonomy: Arcadia's assistive-first policy and hospitals' reluctance about fully autonomous AI ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/hospitals-hesitant-to-adopt-fully-autonomous-ai/)) mean human review before any care action is a selling point, not a weakness. What remains unknown is material. There is no public RFP rubric for generative-AI analytics. There is no current conversion rate for analytics pilots; the 60-70% figure is a 2017 self-reported founder survey ([The Growth Syndicate](https://www.thegrowthsyndicate.com/resources/selling-to-hospitals)). Competitor PMPM prices are not public, and it is not documented what data Innovaccer's, Arcadia's or Lightbeam's copilots can actually query.

## Conclusion

The competitive gap is not intelligence but accountability. The horizontal platforms have shown that verified queries and governed metrics take natural-language analytics to about 90%, but they leave the healthcare curation to the buyer and grade themselves on unreleased suites. The healthcare platforms have the semantics and the agents but publish no evidence that conversational answers are right. An MVP that arrives with VBC definitions already curated, refuses rather than guesses, and hands over a held-out, repeated-run, abstention-scored scorecard on the buyer's own questions competes on a dimension no incumbent has entered. It also turns the most-cited cost of competitors, curation effort, into its own onboarding asset.

The implication for sequencing is that the evidence pack is product, not marketing. The hand-labelled answer sample, the frozen VBC question set and the per-rung cost accounting matter more than the next model or method. They are what clear the governance committee, what the 60-90 day pilot's conversion clause is scored against, and what makes the cost story verifiable. Without them, the stack's verification design is a claim that sounds like everyone else's. With them, it is a receipt that only Agere can show.
