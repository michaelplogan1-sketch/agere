# Hospitals ask regulated questions of unregulated data

Hospitals and health systems need to answer a few hundred recurring questions from their own data. A minority of them have a binding external definition: a CMS rule, a CDC NHSN protocol, a Joint Commission or Leapfrog specification, an HFMA MAP Key, or a state statute. The rest are operational and financial questions defined locally and benchmarked, if at all, against vendor or consultant data. A governed catalogue therefore has to store more than each measure's formula. It also needs the definition steward and version, the source systems and timestamps it depends on, and a computability class: computed by the hospital itself, computed by CMS from claims and only approximated internally, returned by a survey vendor, or a derived composite. The regulatory centre of gravity is moving quickly toward hospital-computed eCQMs. The Hospital IQR eCQM set grows from 8 in CY2026 to 14 in CY2028 and 15 in CY2030, all Hospital Harm eCQMs become mandatory after two self-select years and then feed the Star Rating, and Medicare Advantage patients are folding into every claims-based outcome measure ([HFMA FY2027 IPPS summary](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf)). Three other changes alter the question set: a new composite ED timeliness eCQM targets boarding, sepsis readmissions join the Readmissions Reduction Program, and federal equity and social-needs screening measures have been removed. The fastest-growing questions are on finance: denials, payer mix and expense growth, at a time when about 43% of hospitals were operating at a loss in August 2026 ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/why-43-of-hospitals-are-losing-money/)). No public source ranks which questions hospital teams ask most. The prioritisation below is inferred from mandates, penalties, leader surveys and one case study in which self-service cut custom report requests by 90%.

## Four computability classes sort the regulatory measure set

Every external measure a hospital buyer will ask about falls into one of four classes, and the class determines what an analytics product can honestly promise.

- **Hospital-computed and submitted:** the official value comes from the hospital's own data, so the warehouse can reproduce it exactly and monitor it in near real time.
- **CMS-computed from claims:** the hospital can only approximate the result. It cannot see post-discharge events at other hospitals or CMS's risk models, and it no longer sees the full cohort, because Medicare Advantage encounters now enter most cohorts.
- **Survey-vendor:** the hospital supplies a sample frame and gets scores back.
- **Derived composites:** these can be simulated from published methods using inputs from the other three classes.

The classification below synthesises the CMS rule summaries and program pages cited throughout ([HFMA FY2027 IPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf); [HFMA CY2026 OPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/510110_CY_2026_OPPS_ASC_Final_Rule_Summary.pdf)).

| Class | Measures | What the warehouse can do |
|---|---|---|
| A. Hospital-computed | All IQR, Promoting Interoperability and ORYX eCQMs; OQR ECAT and STEMI eCQMs; SEP-1 and chart-abstracted OQR measures; hybrid core clinical data elements; NHSN HAIs and HCP influenza vaccination; structural attestations; weekly NHSN respiratory data; Leapfrog survey inputs; price-transparency allowed amounts; state discharge abstracts and registries | Reproduce the official value and monitor it continuously |
| B. CMS claims-computed | MORT-30 (six conditions), HRRP readmissions (seven, including sepsis), EDAC (four), COMP-HIP-KNEE, hybrid HWR/HWM final scores, Inpatient Surgical Complications Mortality, PSI 90 and components, MSPB, OQR/REH claims measures | Approximate on the hospital's own encounters for early warning; validate against the hospital-specific reports CMS sends |
| C. Vendor or survey | HCAHPS, OAS CAHPS, THA/TKA PRO-PM, Information Transfer PRO-PM | Supply the sample frame and collect PROMs; ingest returned scores |
| D. Derived composites | VBP Total Performance Score, HACRP Total HAC Score, HRRP payment factor, Star Rating, Leapfrog grade, Birthing-Friendly designation | Simulate from published methodology plus national benchmark files |

Class B results lag reality by one to three years. FY2027 HRRP penalties rest on 2022–2023 discharges, and the Fall 2026 Leapfrog grade uses PSI data from July 2022 to June 2024 ([HFMA FY2027 IPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf); [Leapfrog Fall 2026 measures](https://www.hospitalsafetygrade.org/media/file/Safety-Grade-Reporting-Periods-Fall-2026-ANTICIPATED.pdf)). The commercially valuable question is "what will our penalty be in two years, given current care?", and it can only be answered by mirroring CMS logic on current internal data. The approximation has an unknown error. No primary source quantifies how closely internal replication matches CMS readmission or mortality results. The only figure found is that about 75% of returns for surgical complications go back to the discharging hospital ([HFMA FY2027 IPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf)). A catalogue should label every Class B answer as an estimate and show the gap against the most recent CMS reference value.

### CMS hospital programs: what each measures, and what failure costs

The penalty structure explains why buyers prioritise certain questions. IQR is pay-for-reporting. Hospitals that fail it lose one-fourth of the annual payment update, and about 100 hospitals fail each year. HRRP penalises up to 3% of base operating DRG payments: for FY2027, 2,358 of 2,832 hospitals (83%) were penalised, averaging 0.48%. HACRP cuts 1% from the worst-performing quartile (721 of 2,891 hospitals for FY2027). Hospital VBP withholds 2% of base DRG payments, about $1.9B, and redistributes it, with an average net adjustment of +0.132% and 51.7% of hospitals positive. Promoting Interoperability failure costs IPPS hospitals three-quarters of the market basket. A critical access hospital that is not a meaningful EHR user is paid 100% rather than 101% of reasonable cost ([HFMA FY2027 IPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf)). OQR failure cuts the outpatient update by 2.0 percentage points, a reporting ratio of 0.9805 for CY2026 ([HFMA CY2026 OPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/510110_CY_2026_OPPS_ASC_Final_Rule_Summary.pdf)).

The composites have published mechanics a product can simulate. The Star Rating weights Safety, Mortality, Readmission and Patient Experience at 22% each and Timely and Effective Care at 12%, and assigns stars by k-means within peer groups. From the 2027 rating, hospitals in the lowest Safety of Care quartile lose one star, which affected 459 of 2,847 hospitals in simulation ([HFMA CY2026 OPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/510110_CY_2026_OPPS_ASC_Final_Rule_Summary.pdf)). That single rule makes "which safety measures put us in the bottom quartile?" one of the highest-value executive questions in 2027. VBP groups measures into four domains: Clinical Outcomes (mortality and COMP-HIP-KNEE), Safety (NHSN HAIs plus SEP-1), Person and Community Engagement (HCAHPS) and Efficiency (MSPB). Responsiveness and Care Transition are unscored for FY2027–2029 and dropped from FY2030 ([HFMA FY2027 IPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf)).

The outpatient and rural programs carry their own question sets. OQR covers claims measures (abdomen CT contrast use, 7-day visits after colonoscopy and outpatient surgery, chemotherapy admissions, breast screening recall), the STEMI and ECAT eCQMs, OAS CAHPS and two PRO-PMs. The CY2027 rule removes the normal-colonoscopy follow-up interval measure ([CMS CY2027 OPPS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-2027-hospital-outpatient-prospective-payment-system-opps-ambulatory-surgical-center-0)). The 38 Rural Emergency Hospitals report quarterly with no payment consequence and may use an REH version of ECAT that counts boarding before transfer ([HFMA CY2026 OPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/510110_CY_2026_OPPS_ASC_Final_Rule_Summary.pdf)). For about 1,350 critical access hospitals, MBQIP submission is the only quality requirement for Flex participation. Its updated core set spans global, patient safety, patient engagement, care coordination and ED domains, with ED measures reported quarterly ([Telligen MBQIP](https://telligen.com/rqita/mbqip-measures)). The MBQIP measure list itself was not retrieved. Every hospital and CAH must also submit weekly respiratory illness data to NHSN as a Condition of Participation ([CMS QSO-25-05](https://cms.gov/files/document/qso-25-05-hospitals-cahs.pdf)). That is a recurring operational extract, not a quality measure.

Price transparency became an analytic obligation in 2026. Where a negotiated rate is a percentage or an algorithm, the machine-readable file must now publish 10th-percentile, median and 90th-percentile allowed amounts. These are computed from the hospital's own 835 remittance data over a 12–15-month lookback, and the count of allowed amounts is published alongside them. Enforcement began 1 April 2026 ([HFMA CY2026 OPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/510110_CY_2026_OPPS_ASC_Final_Rule_Summary.pdf)). Compliance remains uneven: PatientRightsAdvocate found 49.4% of 2,000 hospitals fully compliant in its September 2026 review ([PatientRightsAdvocate](https://www.patientrightsadvocate.org/eighth-annual-hospital-price-transparency-compliance-report-september-2026)).

### Three rule changes reshape the catalogue for 2027–2030

**First, Medicare Advantage now enters CMS cohorts.** MA patients are folding into mortality, EDAC, readmissions, COMP-HIP-KNEE and the new sepsis readmission measure, generally with 2-year windows and risk adjustment on ICD-10 codes rather than HCCs. A hospital replicating CMS internally therefore needs payer-agnostic encounter data, and FFS claims alone no longer reproduce the cohort ([CMS/QRC FY2026](https://qualityreportingcenter.com/globalassets/2026/01/iqr/04.-hiqr_summary-of-fy-2026-final-rule-program-changes_vfinal_508.pdf); [HFMA FY2027 IPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf)).

**Second, ED throughput moves from medians to threshold failures.** ECAT replaces the median ED time for discharged patients (OP-18) and left-without-being-seen (OP-22) from January 2028 ([eCQI ECAT page](https://ecqi.healthit.gov/upcoming-emergency-care-access-and-timeliness-ecat-ecqm-hospital-oqr-program)). CMS also floated it for IQR and VBP in the FY2027 rule. Timestamp quality becomes a public-reporting issue: arrival, roomed, decision-to-admit order, departure and walkout disposition.

**Third, federal equity measurement has largely gone.** The FY2026 rule removed the Hospital Commitment to Health Equity measure, both SDOH screening measures, the HCP COVID vaccination measure and the VBP Health Equity Adjustment ([CMS/QRC FY2026](https://qualityreportingcenter.com/globalassets/2026/01/iqr/04.-hiqr_summary-of-fy-2026-final-rule-program-changes_vfinal_508.pdf); [Coronis Health](https://www.coronishealth.com/blog/2026-ipps-final-rule-the-final-word-on-quality)). Stratification questions persist, but they are now driven by contracts and accreditation rather than IQR (see the executive section below).

One correction belongs in any catalogue's change log. The FY2027 rule removes STK-02, VTE-1 and VTE-2 from CY2028. Some secondary summaries of the proposed rule misreported STK-02 as a new measure ([HFMA FY2027 IPPS](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/510155-FY-2027-IPPS_LTCH-PPS-Final-Rule-Summary.pdf)).

### Joint Commission, Leapfrog and states duplicate CMS more than they extend it

The Joint Commission's 2026 ORYX requirement is small. All acute hospitals and CAHs submit the HH-Hypo and HH-Hyper eCQMs, and large hospitals (≥26 beds or ≥50,000 outpatient visits) with obstetrics add PC-02, PC-06 and PC-07 ([TJC 2026 ORYX snapshot](https://jointcommission-ddsp.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/DCS/pages/1030553620)). PC-08, Timely Treatment of Severe Hypertension, is optional for 2027 ([TJC ORYX 2026](https://jointcommission-ddsp.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/DCS/pages/1030619137)). One Joint Commission page lists PC-01/02/05/06 as required for hospitals with ≥300 births, which conflicts with the current snapshot; the snapshot should govern. Psychiatric hospitals report HBIPS-2 and HBIPS-3, restraint and seclusion hours per 1,000 inpatient hours ([QRC HBIPS-2/3](https://dev01.qualityreportingcenter.com/globalassets/2023/11/iqr/ipffy2025/fy2025/hbips--2-3.pdf)). Because every ORYX eCQM is also a CMS eCQM, one pipeline serves both. On 1 January 2026 the Joint Commission replaced its National Patient Safety Goals with 14 National Performance Goals, covering among others staffing, infection prevention, medication management, suicide risk reduction and "health outcomes for all", and cut more than 700 standards ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/joint-commission-cutting-over-700-hospital-standards-accreditation-overhaul); [LAMMICO](https://www.lammico.com/article/NPG-2026)). Survey evidence for these goals comes mostly from policy and observation, not computed measures. The patient-flow standard LD.04.03.11 does require leaders to measure flow. Surveyors ask what causes boarding beyond 4 hours, though hospitals are not scored on that figure ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/capacity-management/joint-commission-clarifies-qpatient-flow-standardq.html)).

Leapfrog's Safety Grade combines 22 inputs. Seven come from the hospital's own Leapfrog Survey: CPOE, BCMA, ICU physician staffing, two safe practices, total nursing care hours per patient day and hand hygiene. Four are HCAHPS composites, and the outcomes are NHSN HAIs plus CMS PSIs and never-event-type measures ([Leapfrog Fall 2026](https://www.hospitalsafetygrade.org/media/file/Safety-Grade-Reporting-Periods-Fall-2026-ANTICIPATED.pdf)). In spring 2027 PSI 4 is replaced by Failure-to-Rescue ([Leapfrog methodology changes](https://www.hospitalsafetygrade.org/media/file/2026-Hospital_Safety_Grade-Methodology_Changes.pdf)). Leapfrog's 2026 complex-surgery standard sets annual hospital and surgeon volume thresholds for 11 procedures, for example pancreatic resection 20/10, THR and TKR 50/25, and mitral valve 40/20 with STS participation required ([Leapfrog 2026 Complex Surgery](https://ratings.leapfroggroup.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2026%20Complex%20Surgery%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf)). Its BCMA standard requires 95% scanning of both patient and medication ([Leapfrog-Castlight BCMA report](https://leapfroggroup.org/sites/default/files/Files/Leapfrog-Castlight_BCMA_Final.pdf)). These are the clearest cases where a warehouse can compute a submission before the June survey deadline.

State obligations vary most and have no current 50-state compendium. Twenty-six states plus DC mandate adverse-event or serious-reportable-event reporting, mostly on NQF categories ([NQF](https://qualityforum.org/Topics/SREs/State_Variability_Fact_Sheet.aspx)). Washington requires a report within 48 hours; Vermont requires one within 7 days and a root-cause analysis within 60 ([VT PSSIS](https://www.healthvermont.gov/sites/default/files/document/2025%20PSSIS%20Annual%20Report_VDH%20Final.pdf)). New York's Rory's Regulations require hospitals to report adherence to their own sepsis protocols, plus the data needed for state risk-adjusted sepsis mortality, to a state registry whose definitions differ from SEP-1 ([Rory's Regulations](https://endsepsis.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/RSF-Rorys-Regulations.pdf); [PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8423398)). A New York hospital therefore runs two sepsis definitions side by side. Staffing laws add shift-level obligations: comprehensive ratio laws in California and Oregon, ICU-only ratios in Massachusetts and New York, staffing committees in eight states, and mandatory public staffing reporting in Illinois, New Jersey, New York, Rhode Island and Vermont ([AFT/Aiken 2025](https://www.aft.org/hc/fall2025/aiken)).

## Clinical quality questions hinge on timestamps that claims cannot supply

Service-line teams track two kinds of measure: process timing (time zero to antibiotics, door-to-device, door-to-needle, guideline therapy at discharge) and outcome tails (mortality, readmission, LOS). Registry programmes own most of the definitions. Claims codes alone cannot compute nearly any of the timing measures; they need timestamped labs, medication administration and orders, or registry abstraction.

| Service line | Question | Definition source | Threshold or definition | Data needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sepsis | Are we meeting bundle timing from time zero, and how does time to antibiotics distribute? | SEP-1; NY sepsis registry where applicable | Time zero = when all severe-sepsis criteria are first present ([Munson algorithm](https://www.munsonhealthcare.org/media/file/Physician%20Services/Practice%20Pro/Sepsis%20Algorithm.pdf)); severe sepsis = ≥2 SIRS plus suspected infection plus organ dysfunction or lactate ≥2 ([PMC9924005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9924005/table/tbl0001)) | Vitals, labs, orders and MAR with timestamps; suspected-infection note |
| Heart failure | Is guideline therapy, including SGLT2i, prescribed at discharge? | AHA GWTG-HF; Joint Commission HF certification | SGLT2i for HFrEF (AHAHF93) and HFpEF/HFmrEF (AHAHF94, required for certification from 2025) ([AHA](https://heart.org/-/media/Files/Professional/Quality-Improvement/Get-With-the-Guidelines/GWTG-Award-Criteria/2025-Recognition-Documents/HFRecognition2025112023.pdf?sc_lang=en); [TJC](https://www.jointcommission.org/resources/news-and-multimedia/newsletters/newsletters/joint-commission-online/sept-18-2024/performance-measure-changes-for-advanced-certification-in-heart-failure-for-2025)); rural HF composite ≥60% ([AHA 2026 Rural](https://www.heart.org/-/media/Files/Professional/Quality-Improvement/Recognition-Criteria/2026/2026-Rural-HF-Recognition-Criteria-61125.pdf?sc_lang=en)) | Discharge med reconciliation, LVEF, contraindications |

| Stroke | What share of door-to-needle times are ≤60, ≤45 and ≤30 min? | AHA Target: Stroke Phase III | Honor Roll ≤60 min in ≥75%; Elite Plus ≤45 in ≥75% and ≤30 in ≥50%; door-to-device ≤90 direct / ≤60 transfer in ≥50% ([AHA](https://www.heart.org/en/professional/quality-improvement/target-stroke/target-stroke-honor-roll)) | Last known well, arrival, CT time, thrombolytic MAR, groin puncture, NIHSS |

Two data-quality traps recur in these clinical measures. Transfusion procedure coding has only about 65% sensitivity, which is why California's maternal quality collaborative tells coders to apply transfusion codes to every maternity case ([CMQCC](https://cmqcc.org/system/files/Appendix%20EE%20Alert%20on%20Blood%20Transfusion%20Procedure%20Coding.pdf)). Ten of 20 SMM indicators also shifted significantly at the ICD-10 transition, so trends across code-set breaks are invalid without adjustment ([PMC5546569](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5546569/table/pone.0182343.t001)). HBIPS rates show 5–10-fold outliers in about 10% of hospitals, partly because some enter hours in the days field ([Psychiatric Services 2020](https://ps.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.202000011)). That is an automatable validity check. SEP-1 itself is contested: IDSA called its criteria "complex and heterogeneous" and proposed antibiotics within 1 hour of septic-shock time zero instead ([PMC7024755](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7024755)). Mature teams therefore track time-to-antibiotics as a distribution, separately for shock and for sepsis, alongside the pass/fail bundle.

### Safety analytics run on three incompatible definitional families

The same harm can carry three different numbers, and they are not interchangeable:

- **Code-based AHRQ Patient Safety Indicators.** These depend on POA coding, so they move with documentation. The v2025 software retired PSI 02 ([AHRQ ChangeLog](https://qualityindicators.ahrq.gov/Downloads/Modules/PSI/V2025/ChangeLog_PSI_v2025.pdf)).
- **Surveillance definitions.** NHSN standardised infection ratios are computed against a 2015 national baseline ([NHSN SIR guide](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20201221064851/https://www.cdc.gov/nhsn/pdfs/ps-analysis-resources/nhsn-sir-guide.pdf)). NDNQI unit-level nursing rates are not comparable to the CMS hospital-level Falls with Injury eCQM ([CASRAI](https://www.casrai.org/guides/nurse-sensitive-indicators)).
- **EHR-derived Hospital Harm eCQMs** built from labs, MAR and flowsheets ([QRC CY2026 eCQMs](https://qualityreportingcenter.com/globalassets/2026/05/iqr/ecqm_cy-2026-available-ecqms_jan2026_vfinal_508.pdf)).

For several years hospitals will see claims-based PSI 12 and PSI 11 alongside EHR-based HH-Postoperative VTE and HH-Respiratory Failure for the same harms. Reconciling them is a natural internal question, because PSIs measure coding as much as care. Rising PSI 90 rates usually call for CDI attention first ([ICD10monitor](https://icd10monitor.medlearn.com/increasing-ahrq-psi-90-rates-requires-facility-attention)).

Two vendor families sit on top of these. Vendor mortality indices depend on proprietary risk models: Vizient's aggregate mortality index fell from 0.97 in 2019 to 0.65 in 2025, while another AHA report sets 2019 at 1.0, so model version and cohort must be pinned before trending ([AHA Aug 2025](https://www.aha.org/system/files/media/file/2025/08/New-Analysis-Connects-Better-Outcomes-for-Hospitalized-Surgical-Patients-to-Improvements-in-Key-Safety-Indicators.pdf)). Trigger-based detection, using the IHI Global Trigger Tool and medication triggers such as naloxone or vitamin K for high INR, finds about 10 times more adverse drug events than voluntary reporting at one site ([PMC8713708](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8713708/); [IHI GTT](https://www.ihi.org/resources/pages/tools/ihiglobaltriggertoolformeasuringaes.aspx)). It is the internal complement to the harm eCQMs.

Three other clinical domains need explicit definitions. Antimicrobial stewardship has the most standardised internal metric: NHSN days of therapy per 1,000 days present, where each agent counts once per calendar day, and the SAAR, a ratio of observed to predicted days for up to 47 location-by-agent combinations. Raw rates should not be compared across facilities ([CDC SAAR training](https://www.cdc.gov:443/nhsn/pdfs/training/2020/saar-508.pdf)). Clinical documentation integrity programmes track review, query, response and agreement rates plus CMI ([AHIMA CDI KPIs](https://journal.ahima.org/AMP_EDN/383/Clinical-Documentation-Integrity-Key-Performance-Indicators-Practice-Brief-2492.amp.html)), but query-rate denominators are not standardised: rates fall when total admissions is the denominator ([ICD10monitor](https://icd10monitor.medlearn.com/the-query-conundrum)). Because coded severity drives expected mortality in vendor O/E indices, analysts routinely split "mortality improvement" into a documentation component and a care component. End-of-life questions (palliative consult timing, hospice referral, ICU use in the final admission) rest on observational evidence that consultation is associated with lower in-hospital death (IRR 0.57) and more hospice referral (IRR 1.45) ([PMC9303815](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9303815)). No single steward defines them.

The notes did not source several measures teams commonly track: the STS CABG composite, AJRR joint-registry measures, pediatric Solutions for Patient Safety harm measures, deterioration metrics (rapid-response calls, unplanned ICU transfers, early-warning-score alert-to-action time) and lab and imaging utilisation. They belong in a catalogue as locally defined measures until a steward's specification is attached.

## Flow, ED and perioperative questions share one timestamp backbone

### ED throughput

The ED's operational vocabulary comes from the Emergency Department Benchmarking Alliance. Its timestamps are arrival, practitioner contact, decision to admit, decision to transfer and ED departure. Its intervals are door to practitioner, door to decision, boarding (decision to departure) and ED LOS, all reported as medians ([ACEP Now, Augustine 2026](https://www.acepnow.com/article/to-optimize-patient-flow-we-need-accurate-standardized-data/)). ECAT now sits alongside those medians as visit-level failure flags. The boarding start time is contested. In a March 2026 comment letter the EDBA recommended starting the boarding clock at the earliest electronic evidence of admission intent, admission order or bed request, and reporting total minutes ([EDBA letter](https://oncprojectracking.healthit.gov/support/secure/attachment/102286/EDBA%20Recommendations%20for%20Acumen%203.30.26.docx)). A governed catalogue should store every candidate timestamp and record which one anchors each interval.

Boarding deserves top priority. Each 10 minutes of boarding was associated with about a 0.1% rise in left-without-being-seen and 3 more minutes of LOS for discharged patients ([ACEP](https://www.acep.org/acep-news/acep-in-the-news-redirects/acep22-researh-forum-coverage/longer-boarding-times-predict-patient-processing-in-ed)). About 68% of admissions arrive through the ED ([ACEP Now](https://www.acepnow.com/article/to-optimize-patient-flow-we-need-accurate-standardized-data/)). Door-to-provider time can be gamed with provider-in-triage contact that does not change care ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/care-coordination/the-quiet-gaps-in-ed-performance-data-and-what-execs-can-do-about-them/)). Ambulance offload is a state mandate in California: the standard is measured from NEMSIS eTimes.11 (arrival) to eTimes.12 (transfer of care), capped at 30 minutes at the 90th percentile, and San Francisco's November 2026 draft tightens it to 20 minutes ([EMSA AB 40 text](https://emsa.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/71/2025/06/AB-40-APOT-Regulations-Reg-Text.pdf); [SF draft](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/EMSA-4000.1-Ambulance-Patient-Offload-Time-Standard-Public-Comment-November-2026.pdf)). The minimum ED data set is therefore:

- Timestamps: arrival, triage, room placement, practitioner contact, admit or transfer decision, bed request, bed assigned, departure.
- Visit attributes: disposition, ESI, arrival mode with EMS times, and a behavioural-health flag for stratified and psychiatric boarding questions.

Benchmarks are dated. Median all-patient ED LOS was 184 minutes in 2023 (EDBA via [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/care-coordination/ed-length-of-stay-falls-in-2023-3-takeaways.html)), and current EDBA medians by disposition were not obtained.

### Inpatient flow and command centres

Inpatient flow metrics have no federal specification. They include occupancy, LOS against the MS-DRG geometric mean (GMLOS), observation hours and conversion, discharge order to departure, bed turnaround and transfer acceptance. Most standard definitions in the notes are practitioner knowledge, not sourced. One evidence finding should shape the catalogue: discharge-before-noon programmes raise the early-discharge share but did not reduce ED boarding or readmissions in a 2024 pre/post study ([ACEP Now](https://acepnow.com/article/hospital-wide-strategies-for-reducing-inpatient-discharge-delays-and-boarding/4)). A governed catalogue should pair "DBN %" with the outcomes it is meant to move rather than report it alone. Command-centre questions are real-time: predicted census by unit at 7am, noon and midnight; beds opening today; medically ready patients waiting; transfer declines for capacity; surge triggers; boarders past 4 hours. Johns Hopkins reported OR holds down 80%, ED boarding down 20% and accepted transfers up 60% after opening its centre in 2016 ([HealthLeaders](https://www.healthleadersmedia.com/innovation/4-insights-build-better-command-center)). These are self-reported figures without baselines. The questions need event-level ADT with unit, bed and level of care; order timestamps; environmental-services bed-status events; transfer-centre logs; and care-management avoidable-day codes.

### Perioperative operations

The definitional standard for OR timestamps is the AACD Procedural Times Glossary, revised in 2018. Turnover runs from the prior patient out of room to the next patient in room (item 2.22), and start time is patient-in-room ([Boggs, Tsai, Urman 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10916-018-1022-6)). Almost every published utilisation benchmark is vendor or consultant data without a common definition. First-case on-time start has a cited median of 64.3% and 90th percentile of 88.3%, and turnover a survey median of 28.5 minutes, both from dated collaborative data ([OR Manager](https://www.ormanager.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/0112_ORM_5.Benchmark_r.pdf)). Cath and EP labs are targeted at 80–85% utilisation ([CI Today/MedAxiom](https://citoday.com/articles/2025-july-aug/when-is-enough-enough-rethinking-staffing-and-utilization-in-todays-cath-and-ep-labs)). Each computed value must carry its parameters: grace window, raw vs adjusted utilisation, prime-time hours and outlier caps.

| Question perioperative leaders ask | Measure | Data needed |
|---|---|---|
| Which blocks are under-used and should be released or reallocated? | Raw and adjusted block utilisation; release lead time and pickup | Block schedule and release log, patient in/out of room, surgeon and service |
| Why are first cases late, and whose delays are they? | First-case on-time start with delay reason | Scheduled vs actual in-room time, delay codes |
| Are booked durations accurate? | Bias and absolute error by surgeon and procedure | Booked vs actual in-room minutes, CPT. Dexter and Macario recommend monitoring each service's bias ([Can J Anesth 2005](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/BF03022054)) |
| How many day-of cancellations, and which were preventable? | Same-day cancellation rate by reason | Cancellation log with reason codes |
| Where is the pre-op-to-PACU bottleneck? | PACU holds, Phase I/II LOS, OR minutes lost | Perioperative status timestamps, PACU census. PACU end-of-staffing overruns can be predicted ([PMC9172889](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9172889)) |
| Which inpatient cases can move to HOPD or ASC? | Site-of-care migration potential and margin | Case log, CPT, patient class, payer contracts. CMS removes 285 services from the Inpatient Only list in phase 1 and ends the list by 2029 ([AHA](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2025-11-21-cms-issues-cy-2026-opps-final-rule)) |
| Do we meet Leapfrog volume standards for every procedure and privileged surgeon? | Hospital and surgeon annual counts | Case log by Leapfrog code lists, surgeon ID across all sites |
| What are our TEAM episode spend and quality score? | Episode spend vs target price; Composite Quality Score | Claims-based CMS reports, internal cost, LOS and discharge destination. TEAM covers CABG, bowel, LEJR, SHFFT and spine from 2026 ([CMS TEAM](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/team-overview-updated.pdf)) |
| Whose cost per case is highest for the same procedure, and does it buy better outcomes? | Supply/implant cost variation; preference-card waste | Item-level used supplies, implant log, pick lists. One hospital's cards for the same procedure ranged from $400 to $1,600 per case with no outcome difference ([HealthTrust](https://healthtrustpg.com/thesource/cqo/clinically-integrated-supply-chain/its-in-the-cards/)); unused items averaged $1,294 per case in one study ([HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/public-health/data-driven-surgical-supply-lists-cut-waste-cost)) |

TEAM makes perioperative efficiency a value-based-care question. Its Composite Quality Score uses hybrid hospital-wide readmission, PSI 90 and the THA/TKA PRO-PM, with harm and failure-to-rescue measures added from the second performance year ([CMS TEAM quality fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/team-intro-qm-fs.pdf)). LOS, discharge to SNF and readmission now feed a Medicare reconciliation for five episodes. NSQIP supplies risk-adjusted 30-day surgical outcomes from more than 150 abstracted variables, but its Operations Manual is restricted to participants ([NSQIP PUF guide](https://facs.org/media/ekmnc2ge/nsqip_puf_userguide_2023.pdf)). Leader surveys put staffing first: 76% of OR leaders named recruitment and retention their primary concern in a 2024 vendor survey ([LeanTaaS](https://leantaas.com/blog/new-survey-shows-perioperative-leaders-focus-on-staffing-as-nationwide-shortages-continue-into-2024/)).

## Finance questions fail on denominators more than on data

### Revenue cycle

HFMA's MAP Keys are the de facto revenue-cycle standard: 29 KPIs in five groups (Patient Access, Pre-Billing, Claims, Account Resolution, Financial Management). Each has a fixed numerator, denominator and inclusion rules, and the general ledger is the preferred source ([HFMA MAP Keys 2023](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/2023-Edition-MAP-Keys.pdf)). The MAP Keys contain no "initial denial rate". Their AR-5 is a remittance denial rate that counts initial and appeal denials at claim level, excluding patient responsibility and recoupments, and AR-6 measures denial write-offs as a share of net patient service revenue. The initial denial rate (first denials ÷ claims submitted, by volume or dollars) and the overturn rate come from HFMA's separate Claim Integrity Task Force ([HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/guidance/standardizing-denial-metrics-revenue-cycle-benchmarking-process-improvement/)). All three are casually called "the denial rate". An answer engine that conflates them will be wrong in a way finance users notice immediately.

The denominators are where governance pays off:

- **Single-month average daily gross revenue:** days in discharged-not-final-billed (DNFB), final-billed-not-submitted (FBNS) and their sum (DNSP).
- **Three-month average net patient service revenue:** net days in A/R, cash collection percentage, denial write-offs and credit-balance days.
- **Gross patient service revenue:** bad debt, charity and uncompensated care.
- **Case mix index:** Medicare MS-DRG weights applied to all payers ([HFMA MAP Keys 2023](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/2023-Edition-MAP-Keys.pdf)).

Under ASC 606, NPSR is gross revenue minus contractual allowances, charity and the implicit price concession.

Public benchmarks are mostly vendor-derived. Kodiak data show initial denials at 11.65% in 2025, final denials at a median of 2.7%, and the clinical-denial overturn rate slipping to 42.1% ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/finance/despite-better-cash-flow-providers-missed-out-more-revenue-2025-due-increased-payer-denials)). Medicare Advantage initial denials ran 10.1% against 4.2% for traditional Medicare ([HealthLeaders](https://www.healthleadersmedia.com/node/99873)). The only HFMA-published percentiles are from 2016–2018 MAP Award winners, who are high performers: median net days in A/R 39.7, cost to collect 2.7%, bad debt 0.6% ([HFMA MAP Award data](https://www.hfma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/32307.pdf)). They are not current national medians. Payer behaviour dominates leader concern: 88% of HFMA/Guidehouse respondents named it the top revenue-cycle stressor, and about 74% reported more prior-authorisation delays. These figures come from a PDF text extraction that should be verified against the original ([Guidehouse/HFMA 2026](https://guidehouse.com/-/media/new-library/industries/health/documents/2026/health-revenue-cycle-management-report2026327.pdf)). To answer denial questions, the data model needs claim-level 837 and 835 records with CARC/RARC reason codes, a first-denial flag, appeal outcome, write-off linkage and the authorisation log. Underpayment analysis adds contract terms and a contract-modelling engine. No public underpayment benchmark was found.

### Margin, cost and volume

Kaufman Hall's National Hospital Flash Report, drawn from more than 1,300 hospitals, is the best public template for the cost question set ([KH NHFR April 2026](https://www.kaufmanhall.com/sites/default/files/2026-06/KH-NHFR_Report-April-2026-Metrics.pdf)). It covers:

- **Profitability:** operating margin and operating EBIDA margin indices, with and without allocations.
- **Revenue:** NPSR per adjusted discharge and per adjusted patient day.
- **Expense:** labor, supply, drug and purchased-service expense, each per adjusted discharge, plus FTEs per adjusted occupied bed.
- **Payer-mix pressure:** bad debt and charity as a share of gross revenue.
- **Volume:** discharges, adjusted patient days, observation share, ALOS, ED visits and OR minutes per calendar day.

Its April 2026 calendar-year-to-date median operating margin was 2.5% including allocations and 8.3% without. That 6-point gap means "operating margin" at hospital level depends on whether system overhead and physician subsidies are allocated, and an answer must state which convention it uses. Secondary reports of the same index conflict (1.7% for March, 2.5% mid-year, 2.9% "without allocations") for exactly this reason. Rating-agency medians are obligor-level and skew to rated systems, so they should never be blended with the Kaufman Hall hospital median: Fitch put FY2025 operating margin at 1.5% with about 212 days cash ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/fitch-2025-likely-brief-operational-peak-nonprofit-hospitals-ahead-obbba-changes)), and Moody's at 1.2% with 188 days ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/nonprofit-hospital-profitability-softened-in-2025-10-notes-from-moodys/)).

Uncompensated care has two competing official definitions:

- **MAP Key FM-4 (charges basis):** bad debt plus charity plus uninsured discounts, divided by gross revenue.
- **CMS cost report Worksheet S-10 (cost basis):** charity care cost plus non-Medicare bad debt cost, converted with the cost-to-charge ratio and excluding Medicaid and Medicare shortfalls ([Noridian S-10](https://med.noridianmedicare.com/documents/10538/3599098/Worksheet+S-10+032024.pdf)).

S-10 drives Medicare DSH uncompensated-care payments, so it has dollar consequences. Contribution margin by service line, DRG or physician; LOS cost impact; 340B savings; and cost per case are internal cost-accounting questions with no public benchmark. They require a cost-accounting system plus GL, payroll by job code, the supply item master and charge detail. Market share and leakage need external all-payer or state discharge data and form a separate data-dependency class. In one older executive survey, 23% did not know how much revenue they lose to leakage ([Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/losing-patients-other-providers-can-cost-some-healthcare-facilities-10-percent-their-revenue)).

The CFO question set these sources imply is inferred, not ranked by any published study:

- Initial and final denial rates by payer, reason and service line, and the preventable share.
- Underpayment against contract.
- Why margin moved: volume, rate, payer mix or cost per adjusted discharge.
- The effect of 2027 Medicaid cuts on payer mix, bad debt and charity.
- Where DNFB, FBNS and A/R over 90 days are stuck.
- Negative-margin service lines and the excess-LOS cost behind them.
- How we compare with Kaufman Hall and rating-agency medians, stated with convention and data year.

## Workforce, pharmacy and supply questions need the widest data joins

### Nursing and workforce

Nursing analytics turn on four linked questions. Do we have enough of the right staff on each unit and shift? What are we paying to fill the gaps? Are we losing people? Are outcomes or regulatory exposure affected?

The core structural measure is nursing hours per patient day. Its numerator is productive hours actually worked by RNs, LPNs and unlicensed assistive personnel who spend more than half their shift in direct care, excluding vacation, orientation and education time ([CHA NDNQI guide](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/1_2018-Data-Collection-Guide-for-Nursing-Sensitive-Measures-.pdf)). Its denominator is midnight-census patient days, which must match the patient days reported to NHSN ([CDC NHSN Nurse Staffing protocol](https://www.cdc.gov/nhsn/psc/nshi/Nurse-Staffing-Indicator-Protocol508c.pdf)). Leapfrog grades the same concept as total nursing care hours per patient day. NDNQI unit-type percentiles are member-only, so a product can compute the measure locally but must flag external comparison as requiring a licence.

NSI's 2026 report, drawn from 527 hospitals and read via secondary coverage, gives the public turnover and vacancy benchmarks:

- RN turnover 17.6%.
- Turnover cost $60,090 per bedside RN ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/workforce/the-cost-of-nurse-turnover-in-10-points-2026/); [nurse.org](https://nurse.org/news/how-much-does-nurse-turnover-cost/)).
- RN vacancy 8.6%, after a methodology change that makes year-over-year comparison directional only ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/nursing/the-current-state-of-nursing-2026/)).

Ratio-law compliance in California and Oregon cannot be answered from monthly hours per patient day. It needs a unit × hour × job-code join of timekeeping, scheduling assignments, hourly census and acuity. The staffing-outcome link that justifies investment comes from Aiken's work: each additional patient per nurse is associated with about 7% higher odds of in-hospital mortality and failure to rescue ([AFT/Aiken 2025](https://www.aft.org/hc/fall2025/aiken)). Workplace violence is a measurable safety outcome: 27% of 2,090 surveyed nurses were physically assaulted in 2025 ([nurse.org](https://nurse.org/articles/nurses-workplace-violence-2026)). It needs incident-reporting data normalised per patient days or FTEs. Leaders rank recruitment and retention first (49%) and staffing second (37%) ([AONL 2025](https://www.aonl.org/system/files/media/file/2025/03/AONL-Nursing-Leadership-Insight-Study-2025.pdf)).

### Provider operations

Provider operations questions centre on wRVUs per clinical FTE against MGMA percentiles. Primary care top-quartile physicians produced more than 7,585 wRVUs and bottom-quartile fewer than 4,165 ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/make-more-money-by-making-your-doctors-more-productive-mgma-expert-says)). MGMA's 2026 data show compensation rising while productivity falls ([Physicians Practice](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/physician-pay-productivity-split-mgma-report)). A 2026 fee-schedule change to wRVUs means some of the year-over-year "decline" may be an artefact, which a governed answer must flag. Third-next-available appointment, the IHI-originated access measure, has no national standard and contested validity ([NAM 2015](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/20220/chapter/4)).

### Pharmacy

Pharmacy questions are the most cross-source in the hospital:

- **Shortages:** wholesaler fill rates plus dispensing and MAR substitution evidence. ASHP counted 225 active shortages in Q3 2026, against a peak of 323 in Q1 2024 ([ASHP](https://www.ashp.org/Drug-Shortages/Shortage-Resources/Drug-Shortages-Statistics)). Managing them takes about 1.5 FTE in large hospitals ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/hospitals-increasingly-tap-pharmacists-clinical-care-struggle-fill-rolls-survey)).
- **Diversion surveillance:** reconciles automated-dispensing-cabinet withdrawals with administrations, waste and who was on shift.
- **340B savings and compliance:** need separate 340B and GPO purchasing accounts plus encounter-level eligibility. 340B is under policy pressure: 60% of pharmacy leaders expect a manufacturer rebate model ([Managed Healthcare Executive](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/pharmacy-leaders-in-ashp-survey-worry-about-340b-program-changes)), which makes "how much 340B savings is at risk?" a likely 2026–2027 executive question.
- **BCMA scanning compliance:** has the only external standard in this group, Leapfrog's 95% ([Leapfrog-Castlight](https://leapfroggroup.org/sites/default/files/Files/Leapfrog-Castlight_BCMA_Final.pdf)).

Drug expense per adjusted discharge rose about 6% year-to-date in 2026 ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/43-of-hospitals-are-in-the-red-6-financial-notes/)). Inventory turns, waste, administration timeliness, formulary adherence and IV-room throughput have no sourced definition or benchmark and belong in the catalogue as locally defined measures.

### Supply chain

The supply chain's standard measure comes with an explicit warning from its own steward. AHRMM defines supply expense as the net cost of tangible expensed items including freight and tax, minus rebates and excluding labor. It pairs supply expense per CMI-adjusted discharge with quality and outcome measures, and it calls the standalone per-adjusted-discharge figure "no longer viable" for benchmarking ([AHRMM CQO](https://www.ahrmm.org/resource-repository-ahrmm/cqo-metrics-100114-1?page=5)). Supply expense per adjusted discharge was up about 5% year-to-date in 2026 ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/43-of-hospitals-are-in-the-red-6-financial-notes/)). Implant recall response and surgeon-level preference-item variation depend on UDI capture in OR logs, which is often incomplete. A product should report missing UDI as a known data-quality condition rather than produce a false zero.

## Executive and equity questions are defined locally, so definition governance is the product

### Board and C-suite dashboards

Published board guidance agrees on structure more than content. The AHA recommends about 10–25 measures, reviewed at least quarterly, with directors probing root causes of trends and variances ([AHA Trustee Services](https://trustees.aha.org/sites/default/files/trustees/Best_practices_dashboards.PDF)). Governance commentary advises leading with risk-adjusted mortality, HAIs and patient experience, tracking leading as well as lagging indicators, and warns that custom scorecards can weaken oversight ([NRC Health](https://nrchealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/SysFcs_Evaluating-Board-and-Committee-Dashboards_Dec2019_Krein.pdf)). IHI links effective quality governance to structure, such as having a quality committee and putting quality on the board dashboard ([IHI](https://www.ihi.org/sites/default/files/IHIResearchSummary_EffectiveBoardGovernanceHealthSystemQuality.pdf)). No authoritative body publishes a standard metric list, so every system builds its own scorecard and the same metric name can mean different things across systems. Boards also ask "why did this change?" as often as "what is it?", which makes decomposition and drill-down a core workflow rather than a feature.

### Equity stratification

Equity stratification survives in four places after the federal removals:

- **CMS confidential reports.** CMS still sends hospital-specific reports with readmissions stratified by dual eligibility and race/ethnicity, but they are not public and do not affect payment ([CMS HRRP](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/prospective-payment-systems/acute-inpatient-pps/hospital-readmissions-reduction-program-hrrp)).
- **HRRP peer grouping.** Penalties are set against peer groups defined by dual-eligible share. This is the one payment program where social-risk stratification moves dollars.
- **Joint Commission.** The equity standard required an equity leader, social-needs screening, stratified quality data and an action plan ([TJC 2023](https://jointcommission.org/resources/news-and-multimedia/news/2023/01/the-joint-commission-elevates-health-care-equity-standard-to-national-patient-safety-goal)). It was folded into the National Performance Goals in 2026, and whether every element survived was not confirmed from Joint Commission text ([McDermott Will & Schulte](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/joint-commission-announces-key-accreditation-updates-for-2026/)).
- **Health-plan contracts.** NCQA HEDIS race/ethnicity stratification now follows OMB SPD 15, including the MENA category from MY2026, on about 22 measures ([NCQA](https://ncqa.org/health-equity/data-and-measurement)). New York Medicaid VBP requires at least one stratified HEDIS measure in every Level 1+ contract ([NY DOH](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/medicaid/redesign/vbp/quality_measures/2026/tcgp_qms.htm)), passing stratification down to provider organisations.

Requests like "show me measure X by race, language, payer, dual status or deprivation index" will persist, with less uniform definitions. A governed answer must state its denominator and report unknown or declined race as an explicit group. NCQA's MY2027 changes target exactly that gap ([NCQA MY2027](https://www.ncqa.org/blog/hedis-my-2027-whats-new-whats-changed-whats-retired/)).

### Patient experience, access and population health

Patient experience questions now include the three new HCAHPS measures: Care Coordination, Restfulness and Information about Symptoms. They apply to discharges from January 2025 and are publicly reported from October 2026, while Care Transition was removed ([PRC](https://prcexcellence.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/Patient_CAHPS/FINAL-HCAHPS-survey-changes-for-January-2025-002.pdf)). Access questions cover third-next-available appointment, no-shows, referral completion and leakage. Population health and VBC questions cover attributed lives, PMPM cost, shared-savings projection, quality-gate measures, HCC recapture, and ED visits and admissions per 1,000. Post-acute questions cover discharge-to-SNF rates and SNF readmissions by facility. These questions connect the hospital catalogue to outcome-aligned payment, but most need claims from outside the system.

### Demand evidence and the hardest questions

The evidence on question demand is thin, but it points one way. OSU Physicians fielded about 120 ad hoc report requests a month with 2 FTEs, mostly on physician productivity, quality and patient satisfaction by provider or location. After a role-based self-service rebuild, custom requests fell 90% ([Physicians Practice](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/how-one-health-system-turned-data-chaos-into-a-90-drop-in-report-requests)). That suggests most ad hoc demand consists of variants of standard questions. The same case named the two hardest problems: the HR employee-ID to NPI crosswalk, and the ambiguity of "patient" versus "encounter" (appointment vs billing encounter, distinct patients vs visits). Clinical data abstractors spend more than half their time on manual entry, per a vendor-sponsored survey ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2024/08/13/clinical-data-abstractors-overburdened-by-manual-tasks)), and less than 60% of health systems' data informs business decisions ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechanalytics/news/366590225/Data-Utilization-Investment-Lagging-Among-Hospitals-and-Health-Systems)).

The hardest question classes follow from these obstacles, in order:

1. Leakage and total cost of care, which need outside claims.
2. Post-acute network performance, which needs outside facility outcomes.
3. Stratified equity gaps, which suffer from missing demographics and small cells.
4. Abstracted measures such as SEP-1 and perinatal care.
5. Staffing per encounter, which depends on the HR-to-EHR crosswalk.
6. "Why did X change?", which needs a stable definition plus decomposition.

When the data are absent, as with outside claims, a supported "unknown" is a legitimate answer.

### What differs by hospital type

Hospital type changes the obligations:

- **Critical access hospitals** answer to MBQIP and the 101% cost-based meaningful-use rule rather than to HRRP, HACRP or VBP. ORYX requires only the two glycaemic eCQMs ([TJC CAH 2026](https://jointcommission-ddsp.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/DCS/pages/1030619150)). Rural HF recognition has its own criteria ([AHA 2026 Rural](https://www.heart.org/-/media/Files/Professional/Quality-Improvement/Recognition-Criteria/2026/2026-Rural-HF-Recognition-Criteria-61125.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Rural Emergency Hospitals** report a short claims-and-ED set with no payment consequence.
- **Multi-hospital systems** must handle ECAT weighting across multi-ED CCNs, the allocation convention behind hospital-level margins, Leapfrog surgeon volumes counted across all sites, and network transfer balancing.
- **Academic medical centres** are benchmarked against vendor AMC risk models, such as Vizient's 2023 AMC mortality model ([AHA](https://www.aha.org/system/files/media/file/2025/08/New-Analysis-Connects-Better-Outcomes-for-Hospitalized-Surgical-Patients-to-Improvements-in-Key-Safety-Indicators.pdf)), whose covariates are proprietary.

## Conclusion

Three things decide whether a hospital question catalogue can be trusted, and none of them is the measure list. The first is provenance. Every entry must carry its steward, specification version, data window and computability class, because the same harm already has claims, surveillance and eCQM versions, and the same "denial rate" or "operating margin" has three or more defensible definitions. The second is the timestamp spine. ECAT, SEP-1, the Hospital Harm eCQMs, hybrid measures, OR efficiency, ratio-law compliance and command-centre forecasting all rest on the same event-level chain: arrival, order, administration, result, room, bed and departure times. One well-governed ADT, order and MAR timestamp layer answers a disproportionate share of the catalogue, and it is where CMS is moving public reporting. The third is honest scope. Class B claims measures, leakage, total cost of care and post-acute performance cannot be computed from a hospital's own warehouse. Answers to those questions should be labelled estimates or supported unknowns, never presented as official values.

These findings also change how demand should be ranked. The evidence suggests buyers do not need more novel analyses. They need the governed recurring questions their teams already ask, answered consistently with stated definitions, plus forward simulation of penalties that CMS will only report two years from now. Those questions cover denials by payer and reason, margin decomposition, boarding, safety quartile position, staffing to ratio, and 340B exposure. The open risks are specification drift and gated benchmarks. Hospital Harm eCQM logic, the MBQIP list, NSQIP and NDNQI definitions, and current EDBA medians were not verified from primary text. Several finance and workforce benchmarks rest on secondary summaries of paywalled reports. A product should treat each of these as versioned, replaceable reference data rather than hard-coded truth.
